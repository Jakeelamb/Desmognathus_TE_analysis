Genome size, cellular architecture and repeat evolution

Eight-figure publication refinement · separate review version

8 main figures · 26 isolated panels. Arial throughout · fixed species order and biological colors · frozen numerical results.

Each full collage is followed by its individual panels. Scroll to inspect every figure, or select an image for its full-size PNG.

Central result: Genome-size estimates and erythrocyte dimensions covary beyond maximum body size. Nuclear and cellular dimensions are not uniformly proportional. Repeat-divergence profiles differ among reproductive groups, while the measured repeat summary does not account for the cellular association.

Reading the arrows: Figure 6 displays coefficients within a proposed causal orientation and the other directions compatible with its independence claims. Relative model support is not a probability that an arrow is causal.

Within-LTR low-divergence representation differs among reproductive groups in an exploratory phylogenetic comparison. (a) All 34 species are shown as gray divergence profiles; colored curves are equal-weight reproductive-group means. Each 1-percentage-point bin contains its percentage of all RepeatMasker-aligned hit bp; bins 0–49 are displayed with missing order/bin combinations zero-filled. (b) All 34 named species retain the shared phylogenetic order. The within-LTR fraction is LTR hit bp in bins 0–4 divided by all LTR hit bp across all divergence bins. It separates relative profile shape from total LTR representation; it is not genome occupancy, insertion age, or transposition rate. (c) Stored Pagel-lambda PGLS predictions from logit(within-LTR fraction) ~ strategy + log10(maximum SVL), restricted to each group's observed SVL range; bands are conditional 95% intervals. (d) Stored raw-logit contrasts and 95% t intervals compare each group with aquatic eggs, before and after SVL adjustment. The adjusted omnibus likelihood-ratio P is 0.000568 (2 df). The adjusted terrestrial-versus-aquatic contrast is 0.439 (95% CI 0.061–0.818); the direct-development contrast is −0.448 (−1.037–0.142). Strategies were expert-coded after PCA inspection; group sizes are not counts of independent evolutionary transitions. Contrasts remain exploratory and conditional on the source tree and fitted lambda. The within-LTR fraction covaries with dnaPipeTE unresolved fraction (unadjusted Spearman rho = −0.452, P = 0.0072), but not clearly with aligned sample fraction (rho = −0.161, P = 0.362). These cross-pipeline quality associations do not establish the origin of the group pattern. Abbreviations: LTR, long terminal repeat; SVL, snout–vent length; PGLS, phylogenetic generalized least squares; CI, confidence interval.

The terminal/internal proxy does not resolve a genome-size relationship
All selected elements are retained, including extremes. The internal-region definition and mapping limitations prevent interpreting this as a calibrated deletion rate.

The selected LTR terminal/internal depth proxy does not resolve a relationship with genome size. (a) All 380 retained LTR/Gypsy elements from 30 species are shown in the fixed phylogenetic row order; no extra outliers were removed for this refinement. Gray density outlines use a pooled bandwidth in log2-ratio space, equal maximum widths and observed-range trimming. Hollow points show every retained element, jittered vertically only. Blue marks show species medians of raw ratios and released 95% intervals from 2,000 element bootstraps. Right labels give genome size and element count; n/a marks unavailable genome size. The ratio is length-weighted mean per-base depth across both terminal LTRs divided by mean depth across the entire intervening sequence, including zero-depth bases. The frozen primary filter requires elements at least 3,000 bp long, at least five parsed TEsorter domains, inclusive within-species 1.5-IQR fences, and at least 80% positive-depth coverage in each of the three regions. Ratio 1 indicates equal mapped depth; it is not a calibrated no-deletion baseline. This statistic is a mapping-depth proxy, not an ectopic-recombination or DNA-loss rate. Elements are not independent biological replicates. (b) Nineteen overlapping species (239 elements) contribute one median each. Stored log10–log10 Pagel-lambda PGLS predictions are reused unchanged, with slope 0.024196 (95% t interval −0.560865–0.609257; P = 0.931489), lambda approximately zero. The band is a 95% coefficient interval, not a species prediction interval. Gray bars show released measurement intervals, which are not propagated into the regression. The broad slope interval allows associations in either direction. All 19 species are named in the isolated regression panel. Stored Gbp coordinates are divided by 0.978 to display pg/1C; this unit conversion leaves the log–log slope and inference unchanged. Genome size is based on nuclear integrated optical density (IOD), anchored to the D. fuscus assembly scale. Measurement intervals exclude anchor uncertainty; run-specific calibration remains unresolved. Sizes are species summaries, not independently validated C-values. Abbreviations: LTR, long terminal repeat; PGLS, phylogenetic generalized least squares.

Cellular covariation without fixed proportionality
Genome–cell association is the clearest pairwise pattern. Nuclear area increases less steeply in the displayed conditional regression; paired nuclear fractions vary among species.

Genome size covaries with erythrocyte area, while nuclear and cellular dimensions are not uniformly proportional. Genome size and cellular architecture in all 24 sampled species. (a,b) Cell area and nucleus area versus genome size, with log10-standardized Pagel-lambda PGLS fits. β denotes a standardized coefficient; brackets give 95% t intervals; P values are unadjusted. (c) Conditional PGLS of log10 nucleus area on log10 cell area. Its unstandardized slope is compared with slope one; the dashed proportional reference passes through the log-scale species centroid. This regression does not identify an errors-in-variables structural exponent. The measured LTR divergence signature does not account for the genome–cell association in the fitted models. (a) All 21 species with complete repeat, cellular and maximum-SVL data are shown. Five species are labelled in the collage; all 21 are named in the isolated panel. The LTR score is aligned-hit bp in divergence bins 0–4 divided by all LTR aligned-hit bp; it is not genomic TE load, an insertion rate or calibrated age. Solid and dashed lines are the frozen SVL-adjusted and marginal Pagel-lambda PGLS predictions. The adjusted line holds SVL at the 21-species geometric mean (62.13 mm); its gray band is a conditional 95% coefficient interval. Vertical bars show genome-size measurement intervals, which are not propagated into these fits. (b) TE-score coefficients for genome, cell and nuclear outcomes. The vertical bar means adjusted for the listed covariates. The genome coefficient is 0.311 [−0.381, 1.003], P = 0.358. (c) Existing 24-species, matched 21-species and TE-adjusted 21-species coefficients use the same original cellular scaling. Both nuclear equations include genome and cell area; SVL appears in every model and TE is added only to the indicated series. The matched genome–cell coefficient changes from 0.549 to 0.527 with TE adjustment. Wider intervals do not establish absent effects or mechanistic independence. Intervals are t intervals conditional on fitted lambda. Cellular traits are standardized log10 values using the full24 means and SDs; the logit TE fraction uses full34 scaling. D. brimleyorum, D. folkertsi and D. ochrophaeus lack the TE endpoint and are not imputed. Genome sizes are nuclear-IOD-derived and D. fuscus anchored; unresolved staining calibration, selected large-cell sampling and tree provenance remain limitations. Shapes and colors identify reproductive strategy in (a), and species-set/model adjustment in (c). This exploratory comparison does not identify a TE-to-genome-to-cell causal sequence. Abbreviations: TE, transposable element; LTR, long terminal repeat; SVL, snout–vent length; PGLS, phylogenetic generalized least squares.
