# Eight-figure findings and captions

Refinement of frozen results; no models refitted. Figures are descriptive or exploratory as specified below.

## Fig. 1. Repeat architecture across Desmognathus

**Finding:** LTRs dominate classified repeat alignments across all 34 species. These percentages describe classified aligned bases, not whole-genome occupancy.

Classified repeat alignments share an LTR-dominated architecture across the 34 sampled species. (a) Topology-only source species tree for 34 Desmognathus species; branch lengths are omitted. Reproductive strategies are aquatic eggs (7 species), terrestrial eggs with aquatic larvae (24), and direct development (3). (b) Twenty-one species have genome sizes, numeric labels and conditional 95% measurement-bootstrap intervals; n/a marks 13 unavailable estimates. (c) dnaPipeTE classified aligned bases are grouped into LTR, LINE, TIR and remaining classified orders (Other). Each row sums to 100% of this denominator. Unresolved sequence is outside the denominator; these proportions are not whole-genome occupancy. All three panels share the same phylogenetic row order. Genome size is based on nuclear integrated optical density (IOD), anchored to the D. fuscus assembly scale. Measurement intervals exclude anchor uncertainty; run-specific calibration remains unresolved. Sizes are species summaries, not independently validated C-values. Source-tree publication and calibration provenance require resolution before submission. Abbreviations: LTR, long terminal repeat; LINE, long interspersed nuclear element; TIR, terminal inverted repeat.

Alt text: A fixed 34-species row order aligns phylogeny, genome sizes, and LTR-rich repeat composition.

## Fig. 2. LTR divergence, body size and reproductive strategy

**Finding:** Within-LTR normalization separates the low-divergence signature from total LTR representation. Strategy association remains exploratory; the adjusted direct-development contrast is uncertain.

Within-LTR low-divergence representation differs among reproductive groups in an exploratory phylogenetic comparison. (a) All 34 species are shown as gray divergence profiles; colored curves are equal-weight reproductive-group means. Each 1-percentage-point bin contains its percentage of all RepeatMasker-aligned hit bp; bins 0–49 are displayed with missing order/bin combinations zero-filled. (b) All 34 named species retain the shared phylogenetic order. The within-LTR fraction is LTR hit bp in bins 0–4 divided by all LTR hit bp across all divergence bins. It separates relative profile shape from total LTR representation; it is not genome occupancy, insertion age, or transposition rate. (c) Stored Pagel-lambda PGLS predictions from logit(within-LTR fraction) ~ strategy + log10(maximum SVL), restricted to each group's observed SVL range; bands are conditional 95% intervals. (d) Stored raw-logit contrasts and 95% t intervals compare each group with aquatic eggs, before and after SVL adjustment. The adjusted omnibus likelihood-ratio P is 0.000568 (2 df). The adjusted terrestrial-versus-aquatic contrast is 0.439 (95% CI 0.061–0.818); the direct-development contrast is −0.448 (−1.037–0.142). Strategies were expert-coded after PCA inspection; group sizes are not counts of independent evolutionary transitions. Contrasts remain exploratory and conditional on the source tree and fitted lambda. The within-LTR fraction covaries with dnaPipeTE unresolved fraction (unadjusted Spearman rho = −0.452, P = 0.0072), but not clearly with aligned sample fraction (rho = −0.161, P = 0.362). These cross-pipeline quality associations do not establish the origin of the group pattern. Abbreviations: LTR, long terminal repeat; SVL, snout–vent length; PGLS, phylogenetic generalized least squares; CI, confidence interval.

Alt text: All-species repeat profiles and named within-LTR fractions accompany body-size predictions and phylogenetic strategy contrasts.

## Fig. 3. Repeat diversity and genome size

**Finding:** Both indices describe the same ten-superfamily composition. Their agreement is not independent replication; the Shannon interval crosses zero.

Dominant-superfamily evenness tends to decline with genome size across the 21 overlapping species. Twenty-one species with both genome-size and repeat-diversity data are shown in each panel. (a) Shannon entropy and (b) Gini–Simpson index summarize each species' ten most abundant positive classified dnaPipeTE superfamilies, reclosed to sum to one. These related measures represent diversity/evenness of the selected superfamily distribution, not total TE burden or independent replications. Points identify reproductive strategy; horizontal bars show genome-size measurement intervals. All observations contribute to the same full-sample fit per panel. Lines and bands reuse stored Pagel-lambda PGLS fitted means and 95% coefficient intervals without refitting. The Shannon slope is −0.007558 per pg/1C (95% CI −0.015234–0.000118; P = 0.0533), and the Gini–Simpson slope is −0.003623 (−0.006964–−0.000283; P = 0.0350). Both fitted lambdas are 1. P values are unadjusted. Predictor measurement error and tree uncertainty are not propagated. D. aeneus, D. wrighti and D. valtos are labelled in the collage to identify the genome-size extremes and the high-diversity direct developer; label selection does not change the 21-species sample. All 21 species are individually named in isolated panels. Genome size is based on nuclear integrated optical density (IOD), anchored to the D. fuscus assembly scale. Measurement intervals exclude anchor uncertainty; run-specific calibration remains unresolved. Sizes are species summaries, not independently validated C-values. Abbreviations: TE, transposable element; PGLS, phylogenetic generalized least squares; CI, confidence interval.

Alt text: Two compact 21-species plots show repeat diversity against genome size with unchanged full-sample PGLS fits and intervals.

## Fig. 4. LTR depth ratios and genome size

**Finding:** All selected elements are retained, including extremes. The internal-region definition and mapping limitations prevent interpreting this as a calibrated deletion rate.

The selected LTR terminal/internal depth proxy does not resolve a relationship with genome size. (a) All 380 retained LTR/Gypsy elements from 30 species are shown in the fixed phylogenetic row order; no extra outliers were removed for this refinement. Gray density outlines use a pooled bandwidth in log2-ratio space, equal maximum widths and observed-range trimming. Hollow points show every retained element, jittered vertically only. Blue marks show species medians of raw ratios and released 95% intervals from 2,000 element bootstraps. Right labels give genome size and element count; n/a marks unavailable genome size. The ratio is length-weighted mean per-base depth across both terminal LTRs divided by mean depth across the entire intervening sequence, including zero-depth bases. The frozen primary filter requires elements at least 3,000 bp long, at least five parsed TEsorter domains, inclusive within-species 1.5-IQR fences, and at least 80% positive-depth coverage in each of the three regions. Ratio 1 indicates equal mapped depth; it is not a calibrated no-deletion baseline. This statistic is a mapping-depth proxy, not an ectopic-recombination or DNA-loss rate. Elements are not independent biological replicates. (b) Nineteen overlapping species (239 elements) contribute one median each. Stored log10–log10 Pagel-lambda PGLS predictions are reused unchanged, with slope 0.024196 (95% t interval −0.560865–0.609257; P = 0.931489), lambda approximately zero. The band is a 95% coefficient interval, not a species prediction interval. Gray bars show released measurement intervals, which are not propagated into the regression. The broad slope interval allows associations in either direction. All 19 species are named in the isolated regression panel. Stored Gbp coordinates are divided by 0.978 to display pg/1C; this unit conversion leaves the log–log slope and inference unchanged. Genome size is based on nuclear integrated optical density (IOD), anchored to the D. fuscus assembly scale. Measurement intervals exclude anchor uncertainty; run-specific calibration remains unresolved. Sizes are species summaries, not independently validated C-values. Abbreviations: LTR, long terminal repeat; PGLS, phylogenetic generalized least squares.

Alt text: Horizontal violins show all retained elements above a 19-species PGLS comparison with genome size.

## Fig. 5. Genome size and cellular architecture

**Finding:** Genome–cell association is the clearest pairwise pattern. Nuclear area increases less steeply in the displayed conditional regression; paired nuclear fractions vary among species.

Genome size covaries with erythrocyte area, while nuclear and cellular dimensions are not uniformly proportional. Genome size and cellular architecture in all 24 sampled species. (a,b) Cell area and nucleus area versus genome size, with log10-standardized Pagel-lambda PGLS fits. β denotes a standardized coefficient; brackets give 95% t intervals; P values are unadjusted. (c) Conditional PGLS of log10 nucleus area on log10 cell area. Its unstandardized slope is compared with slope one; the dashed proportional reference passes through the log-scale species centroid. This regression does not identify an errors-in-variables structural exponent. Solid lines and gray bands in a–c show back-transformed fitted log-scale means and 95% coefficient bands; point bars show released measurement intervals. Measurement errors are displayed but not propagated into these regressions. (d) Fractions are first computed within each actual paired cell and nucleus, then summarized as animal medians (open circles) and the species arithmetic mean of animal medians (diamonds). Bars are the central 95% range of 1,000 animal-then-paired-cell resamples. All 42 animals and 1,152 pairs remain. Species follow the fixed phylogenetic display order. Seven morphology species have only one animal; their intervals omit among-animal uncertainty. All cellular traits describe selected large erythrocytes and two-dimensional nuclear and cell areas. Genome size is IOD-derived and D. fuscus assembly-anchored; calibration and staining-run uncertainty are not represented by the plotted intervals. Shared colors and shapes identify recorded reproductive strategy in a–c. Gray diamonds mark the three species with no frozen egg-placement assignment (D. brimleyorum, D. folkertsi, D. ochrophaeus); their aquatic-larval development is known and they remain in every fit. The overview labels a few fixed reference species; isolated scatterplots label all 24. Label choice changes no data or fits. The association results do not determine causal direction. Abbreviations: PGLS, phylogenetic generalized least squares.

Alt text: Four panels integrate genome–cell and genome–nucleus relationships, conditional nuclear–cell scaling and paired nuclear fractions, retaining all 24 species.

## Fig. 6. Cellular associations and alternative causal hypotheses

**Finding:** The leading expanded classes preserve the cellular connection but differ in body-size placement. Weighted arrows express a specified hypothesis; equivalent orientations remain possible.

Compatible models preserve a genome–cell–nucleus connection while leaving causal direction and body-size placement unresolved. (a) One genome-first orientation of H5 for 24 species. Arrow widths and labels encode absolute standardized coefficients from cell area ~ genome size, nuclear area ~ cell area, and body length ~ cell area. Directions are hypotheses. (b) Separate conditional checks: cell area ~ genome size + body length and nuclear area ~ cell area + genome size + body length. Points and segments show coefficients and 95% t intervals; grey intervals include zero. These are different equations from panel a. (c) CICc weights across eight candidate classes for 24 species and after excluding D. aeneus and D. wrighti (22 species). Both excluded species are small direct developers; the comparison changes body-size range, development and taxon composition together. Weights sum to one within each cohort and candidate set; they are not probabilities of causal truth. H5 and H7 differ by only 0.624 CICc in full24. Skeletons show adjacency, not arrow direction. The primary independence-test order is b,g,c,n; reversing it changes support, with the leading subset22 classes closely ranked. (d) All four acyclic H5 orientations share a skeleton and no unshielded colliders, hence the same graphical conditional independences. These schematic arrows do not encode effect size. Re-estimated residual covariance can yield numerical differences between reversed regressions. G = genome size; C = erythrocyte area; N = nuclear area; B = species maximum snout–vent length. All log10 traits use fixed full24 means and sample SDs. Regressions use Pagel-lambda covariance; intervals condition on fitted lambda and omit tree and measurement uncertainty. The expanded eight-class comparison has not undergone measurement or tree resampling. Genome-size calibration and selection of large eligible erythrocytes limit biological interpretation. Abbreviations: CICc, C-statistic information criterion with small-sample correction.

Alt text: Four panels show a weighted genome-first hypothesis, three body-adjusted regression coefficients, support for eight candidate classes in 24 and 22 species, and four equivalent causal orientations. Genome–cell and cell–nucleus coefficients are positive after body adjustment; the additional genome–nucleus interval spans zero. H5 and H7 lead in 24 species, whereas H8, H7 and H5 are close in 22. The four H5 orientations cannot be distinguished by graphical conditional independences.

## Fig. 7. Body size and sampling sensitivity

**Finding:** The two smallest direct developers influence organismal associations. Positive genome–cell and cell–nucleus coefficients persist in the stated body-adjusted sensitivities.

Genome–cell associations persist across body-size adjustment, animal weighting and the specified species sensitivity. Body size and sampling sensitivity of the 24-species cellular panel. (a–c) Genome size, cell area and nucleus area versus species maximum snout–vent length (SVL). SVL values are species maxima supplied by the collaborator, not body lengths of the microscopy animals. Both axes are logarithmic with original-unit tick labels. Solid lines are the full 24-species Pagel-lambda PGLS fits; dashed lines are fits excluding D. aeneus and D. wrighti (22 species), restricted to the observed body-size range of each fitted panel. The gray band is the full 24-species 95% confidence band for the log-scale fitted mean, back-transformed; it is not a prediction interval. Vertical bars are released trait measurement intervals. β values and the conditional coefficients in d use identical full 24-species log10 means and SDs. Species, not cells, are the model observations. Displayed P values are unadjusted. Neither SVL uncertainty nor predictor measurement error is propagated. (d) Three conditional regressions show the coefficient on genome size in cell ~ genome + SVL, cell area in nucleus ~ cell + SVL, and genome size in nucleus ~ genome + cell + SVL. Four rows compare released summaries and equal-animal summaries in 24 and 22 species. Whiskers are 95% t coefficient intervals conditional on the fitted phylogenetic parameter. Released morphology pools selected paired objects within species before taking the median. The equal-animal sensitivity takes the median of within-animal medians for morphology; IOD first averages image medians within animal, then averages animals. This median-of-medians morphology definition differs from the arithmetic mean-of-animal-medians nuclear-fraction summaries in Figure5d. The 22-species sensitivity removes exactly the two smallest species and only direct developers; their body size, developmental mode and lineage identity are confounded, so altered significance does not identify a causal mechanism or test differences between slopes. Shared strategy colors retain all 24 points, with three unassigned egg-placement records shown gray. Isolated scatterplots label all species. Cell and nuclear areas refer to selected large erythrocytes; genome size is IOD-derived and D. fuscus assembly-anchored. Seven morphology species and four IOD species have one animal. These sensitivity analyses do not resolve staining calibration, causal direction, or population representativeness. Abbreviations: SVL, snout–vent length; PGLS, phylogenetic generalized least squares.

Alt text: Three body-size scatterplots retain every species and compare 24-species with 22-species fits; a coefficient forest adds animal-weighting and species-panel sensitivity.

## Fig. 8. Repeat divergence and cellular integration

**Finding:** The exact 21-species comparison retains the genome–cell coefficient after TE adjustment. Wide TE intervals leave historical insertion and deletion mechanisms unresolved.

The measured LTR divergence signature does not account for the genome–cell association in the fitted models. (a) All 21 species with complete repeat, cellular and maximum-SVL data are shown. Five species are labelled in the collage; all 21 are named in the isolated panel. The LTR score is aligned-hit bp in divergence bins 0–4 divided by all LTR aligned-hit bp; it is not genomic TE load, an insertion rate or calibrated age. Solid and dashed lines are the frozen SVL-adjusted and marginal Pagel-lambda PGLS predictions. The adjusted line holds SVL at the 21-species geometric mean (62.13 mm); its gray band is a conditional 95% coefficient interval. Vertical bars show genome-size measurement intervals, which are not propagated into these fits. (b) TE-score coefficients for genome, cell and nuclear outcomes. The vertical bar means adjusted for the listed covariates. The genome coefficient is 0.311 [−0.381, 1.003], P = 0.358. (c) Existing 24-species, matched 21-species and TE-adjusted 21-species coefficients use the same original cellular scaling. Both nuclear equations include genome and cell area; SVL appears in every model and TE is added only to the indicated series. The matched genome–cell coefficient changes from 0.549 to 0.527 with TE adjustment. Wider intervals do not establish absent effects or mechanistic independence. Intervals are t intervals conditional on fitted lambda. Cellular traits are standardized log10 values using the full24 means and SDs; the logit TE fraction uses full34 scaling. D. brimleyorum, D. folkertsi and D. ochrophaeus lack the TE endpoint and are not imputed. Genome sizes are nuclear-IOD-derived and D. fuscus anchored; unresolved staining calibration, selected large-cell sampling and tree provenance remain limitations. Shapes and colors identify reproductive strategy in (a), and species-set/model adjustment in (c). This exploratory comparison does not identify a TE-to-genome-to-cell causal sequence. Abbreviations: TE, transposable element; LTR, long terminal repeat; SVL, snout–vent length; PGLS, phylogenetic generalized least squares.

Alt text: A 21-species scatterplot and conditional-coefficient panels show uncertain TE associations and a persistent genome–cell relationship after TE adjustment.
