# Independent AI specialist review: repeat evolution and genome-size mechanisms

Reviewed 2026-09-24. I inspected the rendered Figures 1–4, 11–12, their legends, released numerical tables and integration methods, and verified primary literature. No models, data, figures or manuscript files were changed. Below, `RUN` means `/home/jake/Projects/Desmognathus_TE/outputs/manuscript_story_20260924_01`.

The strongest positive discovery is **a shared broad repeat architecture with lineage-associated variation in LTR sequence-divergence profiles**. Figure 1 shows LTRs dominate the classified dnaPipeTE aligned bases in every sampled species: 50.70% in *D. wrighti* through 67.30% in *D. valtos*. LINE and TIR remain substantial components throughout the radiation. This establishes continuity of the broad repeat repertoire; it does not establish conservation of individual insertions or low turnover. Similar proportions can persist while different families or copies expand and disappear. Conversely, different divergence profiles need not imply different total repeat amounts. These distinctions make the result more informative than either “all genomes are alike” or “development determines TE activity.” The established salamander context is LTR enrichment, particularly Ty3/gypsy, relative to other vertebrates; this dataset extends comparative resolution within one radiation. [Local composition table](../figures/figure_1/analysis/broad_composition.csv); [Sun et al., 2012, primary expansion study](https://academic.oup.com/gbe/article/4/2/168/557286).

**The divergence result survives an important denominator clarification.** The primary endpoint is LTR aligned-hit bp in bins 0–4 divided by all LTR aligned-hit bp. It ranges from 2.11% in *D. organi* to 12.20% in *D. fuscus*. The older all-hit endpoint instead ranges from 0.81% to 5.79% in those species and equals LTR/all-hit representation multiplied by the within-LTR fraction. Figure 12 therefore shows that the signal is not solely a consequence of having more LTR representation among annotated hits. Nevertheless, these are RepeatMasker alignments from assembled repeat contigs, with source hit weighting retained. They are not genomic copy-number inventories. Figure 1's classified dnaPipeTE denominator is different again. Neither fraction can simply be multiplied by genome size to estimate recently inserted gigabases. Even within-LTR normalization retains a biological denominator problem: its value can rise through more low-divergence sequence, less high-divergence sequence, or differential ascertainment of either. [Exact species scores and denominators](../figures/figure_12/analysis/repeat_score_species34.csv); [methods](../figures/figure_12/analysis/README.md).

**Reproductive strategy is associated with the profile, but a direct-development mechanism is not isolated.** The primary within-LTR omnibus strategy test remains positive after maximum-SVL adjustment: likelihood ratio 14.947, 2 df, P=0.000568. However, the adjusted terrestrial-eggs/aquatic-larvae contrast against aquatic eggs is +0.439 logit units [0.061, 0.818], P=0.0244, whereas direct development is −0.448 [−1.037, 0.142], P=0.131. The striking visual ordering must not become a claim that each pair differs. All strategy fits have lambda=1. Seven, 24 and three species are not equivalent numbers of evolutionary transitions, and the three direct developers occupy maximum SVL 29–31 mm, below the other groups' observed ranges. Thus, their body-adjusted contrast depends on a common-slope model across separated ranges. Strategy coding followed PCA inspection; inference is exploratory. Tree calibration provenance remains unresolved, and conditional intervals omit tree and fitted-lambda uncertainty. The near-zero adjusted SVL slope does not establish that body size is irrelevant. [Omnibus tests](../figures/figure_12/analysis/strategy_omnibus_tests.csv); [model `strategy_body`, endpoint `within_ltr`](../figures/figure_12/analysis/regression_coefficients.csv).

**Unresolved sequence is a consequential competing explanation.** The primary score correlates negatively with order-unresolved fraction (Spearman rho=−0.4524, P=0.00722); its correlation with repeat-aligned fraction of the configured sample is weaker (rho=−0.1615, P=0.3615). The first result prevents dismissal of annotation quality as an incidental issue. It does not prove artifact: divergent or lineage-specific repeat sequence may be both biologically meaningful and difficult to classify. The second result cannot certify absence of sampling bias. These are uncorrected cross-pipeline diagnostic associations, not covariates demonstrated to explain the strategy result. The fixed 1.5-Gb quantification sample is not a genome-size denominator. A manuscript should preserve the unresolved-fraction association beside the biological pattern. [Predefined quality associations](../figures/figure_12/analysis/predefined_quality_associations.csv).

**The exact21 integration connects the datasets without demonstrating a TE-to-genome causal bridge.** With maximum SVL included, the within-LTR coefficient for genome size is 0.311 [−0.381, 1.003], P=0.358. Its coefficients for cell area conditional on genome/SVL and nucleus area conditional on cell/genome/SVL are 0.066 [−0.264, 0.396] and −0.065 [−0.478, 0.348]. These wide intervals do not exclude biologically important associations. More positively, the genome–cell coefficient changes only from 0.549 to 0.527 when the score is added to the same 21 species; the original24 coefficient is 0.545. The measured divergence signature therefore does not account for that cellular association in the fitted equations. This is robustness to one measured summary, not proof of mechanistic independence or mediation. Missing TE species are explicitly omitted, including *D. ochrophaeus*, rather than imputed. All outcomes retain IOD calibration and sampling limitations. [Models `genome_body_adjusted`, `cell_te_adjusted`, `nucleus_te_adjusted`](../figures/figure_11/analysis/regression_coefficients.csv); [matched coefficients](../figures/figure_11/analysis/cellular_coefficients_matched_comparison.csv).

**Figure 3 adds a dominance/evenness observation.** Its top-ten-superfamily Shannon slope is −0.00756 per pg/1C [−0.01523, 0.00012], P=0.0533; Gini–Simpson is −0.00362 [−0.00696, −0.00028], P=0.0350. Both use the same 21 species and lambda=1. Because ten positive superfamilies are reclosed, this principally describes concentration among dominant groups, not loss of total TE richness. The two indices are correlated summaries, not independent replications. The negative tendency merits comparison with Wang's positive association across a broader salamander sample, but different phylogenetic scales, sampling and quantification prevent treating it as a refutation. [Figure 3 models](../figures/figure_3/analysis/model_summary.csv); [Wang et al., 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-08127-3).

**Figure 4 does not adjudicate insertion versus deletion.** Its 380 elements characterize a selected LTR/Gypsy subset in 30 species; only 19 species enter the genome-size regression. The log–log slope is 0.0242 [−0.5609, 0.6093], P=0.9315. This is failure to resolve a relationship, not equal deletion rates. Ratios below one are not negative deletion: one is equal mapped depth, without an empirical calibration here. Internal coverage spans the entire inter-LTR interval, whereas Wang bounded internal sequence by the first and last TE domains and checked mapping/read treatments. Domain-filter relaxation alone cannot establish comparability. Selection of structurally complete, domain-rich elements also limits what genomic history is represented. [Model `log_log_lambda`](../figures/figure_4/plotted_data/model_summary.csv); [original local comparison](../../../docs/research/wang_2025_ltr_deletion_comparison.md); [Wang deletion methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-08127-3).

Mechanistically, an inherited LTR-rich background combined with episodic family proliferation and differential retention is plausible. Independent studies support unusually slow small-indel DNA loss and low solo-LTR levels in salamanders relative to other vertebrates, making retention a credible contributor to genomic gigantism. They do not identify the cause of differences among these *Desmognathus*. [Sun et al., small-indel study](https://academic.oup.com/gbe/article/4/12/1340/608259); [Frahry et al., large-deletion study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25608479/). Alternative explanations for the present divergence pattern include substitution-rate differences, family mixtures and assembly/reference sensitivity. Germline TE suppression, developmental selection and recent insertion bursts remain hypotheses because expression, insertion polymorphism and calibrated insertion ages were not measured here.

Suggested manuscript interpretation: “Desmognathus retains an LTR-dominated classified repeat architecture while reproductive lineages differ in the representation of low-divergence LTR sequence. This exploratory association persists after within-LTR normalization and body-size adjustment, but is not resolved into a developmental cause. The measured repeat signature neither explains genome-size differences convincingly nor materially changes the genome–cell association. Together, the data distinguish repeat-profile variation from the unresolved historical balance of DNA gain and loss.”
