# Expert-panel synthesis: what the Desmognathus figures establish

Reviewed 24 September 2026. Three independent AI specialist reviews examined evolutionary cell biology, phylogenetic causal inference, and repeat evolution. This synthesis combines those reviews with a direct measurement-provenance check and inspection of the current figures. It introduces no new analyses and changes no existing figures, data, or review notes.

**Principal finding.** The strongest result is a persistent relationship between genome-size estimates and erythrocyte area, accompanied by cell–nucleus covariation that is not uniformly proportional. Maximum body size does not fully account for these cellular associations. Repeat composition supplies a complementary result: broadly similar LTR-dominated architecture contains substantial variation in sequence-divergence profiles associated with reproductive groups. The data constrain simple causal explanations, but do not establish an ordered sequence from TE activity through genome size to nucleus, cell, and organism.

The contribution goes beyond another genome–cell correlation: the integrated comparison identifies which relationships persist after adjustment, which depend strongly on particular taxa, and which proposed connections between repeat profiles and phenotype remain unresolved. The appropriate biological scope is the sampled species and selected erythrocytes, not every tissue or cell in the genus.

**What the path analysis contributes.** It is a central result and should remain central in the manuscript. It evaluates the conditional relationships implied by explicit biological hypotheses. A model in which maximum body size alone explains all three other traits is incompatible with the full24 observations under the fitted assumptions (global P=.000238). An exclusive genome→nucleus→cell chain also fails its requirement that genome and cell size become independent after conditioning on measured nuclear area (P=.000997 in the original three-trait comparison). This challenges those particular simple accounts; it does not reject all body-size influence or DNA effects on nuclei. Measurement error in nuclear area could also leave residual genome–cell association despite a genuine biological mediation process.

Cell area remains associated with nuclear area when genome size and maximum SVL are included: β=.652 [.074, 1.230]. The additional genome coefficient is .051 [−.499, .600]. The latter interval is too wide to establish a negligible direct effect. A nonsignificant coefficient cannot prove complete mediation.

The observed genome–cell–nucleus connection permits multiple causal orientations with the same conditional-independence implications. In Figure 13, the two leading full24 classes have weights .524 and .384 and differ by only .624 CICc. Both preserve that cellular connection, but attach body size differently. A simpler class can win by its complexity penalty rather than a superior raw fit. These weights are comparisons within the eight candidates, not probabilities that arrows are biologically true. Removing the two direct developers leaves three classes within .626 CICc; changing independence-test order changes their ranking. This is consequential uncertainty about the placement of body size, not a disappearance of all cellular structure. [Expanded comparison](../figures/figure_13/analysis/model_comparison.csv).

Phylogenetic robustness also requires its taxon context. The published-tree comparison contains only 18 species, where both cell-bridge and nucleus-bridge classes remain competitive. The same 18 species on the focal tree already give ΔCICc=.593 between them. Thus, a first-ranked model across 200 trees is not uniquely identified across species coverage. Residual lambda near zero in some regressions does not mean the traits lack phylogenetic history. See the [causal-inference review](causal_inference.md).

**Evolutionary meaning of the cellular pattern.** DNA amount could influence viable cellular dimensions through nucleotypic effects—physical and physiological consequences of DNA quantity rather than the functions of particular genes. The present associations are compatible with that idea, but also with selection on cellular properties constraining genome expansion, or with other lineage characteristics influencing both. These explanations can coexist over evolutionary time: DNA gain can have cellular consequences, and selection can subsequently filter those consequences. An evolutionary cell→genome hypothesis would concern differential retention or selection across generations, not an adult erythrocyte changing its inherited genome simply by becoming larger.

The nuclear result makes a simple proportional enlargement account incomplete. Larger cells in the sampled panel tend to have larger nuclei, but nuclear area increases less steeply in the fitted conditional regression. Multiple cellular determinants of nuclear size are biologically plausible: experiments in Xenopus demonstrated nuclear-size regulation by nuclear-import factors. That supplies a mechanism to consider, not evidence that these same factors explain salamander erythrocytes. [Levy and Heald 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20946986/).

Body dimensions may change through cell number, growth schedules, or tissue-specific processes without matching erythrocyte dimensions exactly. Those routes were not measured here. Likewise, cell size could constrain miniaturization or development, but tissue packing, division rates, metabolic performance, and fitness were not observed. Earlier plethodontid work linked genome size to embryonic developmental time yet could not distinguish adaptive DNA accumulation from fitness constraints on accumulation; our developmental-mode categories do not supply the missing rate or fitness measurements. [Jockusch 1997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1688400/).

**The small direct developers are informative, not disposable outliers.** Aeneus and wrighti are the two smallest species and the only direct developers in the 24-species cellular panel. Their exclusion weakens body–genome and body–cell associations, while genome–cell association remains positive with an interval excluding zero in the reported body-adjusted fits. This localizes sensitivity: the organismal association is more dependent on that part of the sampled trait range. It does not prove that the remaining association is zero or that development caused the difference. Removing the same two species changes size range, developmental composition, and phylogenetic representation simultaneously. Their paired nuclear fractions also overlap those of aquatic-larval species. There is no single demonstrated cellular solution to direct development in these data.

**Repeat evolution supplies a second axis of evidence, without completing the causal chain.** The low-divergence LTR fraction varies approximately 2.1–12.2% when its denominator is all LTR aligned-hit bases. Within-LTR normalization shows the reproductive-group result is not merely a consequence of total LTR representation among hits. The supported adjusted pairwise contrast is terrestrial eggs with aquatic larvae versus aquatic eggs (P=.0244); direct development versus aquatic eggs is unresolved (P=.131). Species counts do not equal independent evolutionary transitions, and strategy coding followed inspection of the composition analysis.

A high low-divergence fraction could reflect more low-divergence sequence, less older sequence, different substitution rates or family mixtures, or differential recovery. The negative association with unresolved sequence (rho=−.452, P=.00722) makes ascertainment a meaningful competing explanation, without proving the pattern is an artifact. Neither these fractions nor the fixed sequencing sample measure absolute recently inserted DNA. Consequently, the failure of this score to explain genome size does not exclude TEs as historical contributors to genome expansion.

The deletion result remains mechanistically unresolved. The current inter-LTR coverage denominator differs from Wang's domain-bounded internal region; mapping comparability is unresolved. A terminal/internal ratio below one is therefore not evidence of negative deletion, and an imprecise null association is not evidence of equal deletion rates. Independent salamander work provides precedent for DNA retention through slow loss, but this dataset has not assigned the differences among these species to that mechanism. [Wang et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-08127-3); [Sun et al. 2012, small-indel loss](https://academic.oup.com/gbe/article/4/12/1340/608259); [Frahry et al. 2015, large deletions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25608479/).

**The most consequential measurement qualification.** The genome-size trait is IOD scaled to a D. fuscus assembly anchor. The released denominator is the fuscus target-species IOD estimate, not recovered run-matched standard-slide values. Exact standard-to-target run mapping and the approximately 41% difference between two standard-slide median IODs remain unresolved. A common multiplicative calibration error changes absolute genome sizes but cancels from standardized log associations; differential staining or batch effects can alter species comparisons and associations. This is why the signal can be statistically persistent without yet constituting fully validated DNA-content variation. [Current morphology interpretation boundary](../../../analyses/02_morphology/README.md).

The large-cell selection rule also defines the biological target: typically the 50 largest eligible erythrocytes per species, with fewer for aeneus and wrighti. Seven morphology species and four IOD species have only one sampled animal. Forty-one animals overlap between assays, but their joint measurement covariance is not modeled by independent assay resampling. These issues qualify all downstream models; additional plots of the same data are not independent replication. Their importance does not erase the observed pattern, but it prevents causal certainty from being inferred from bootstrap stability alone.

**Recommended manuscript argument.** Across sampled Desmognathus, genome-size estimates covary with erythrocyte area beyond differences in maximum body size. Nuclear and cell dimensions covary without fixed proportionality, and model comparisons favor a cellular connection while leaving causal direction and the position of body size unresolved. A shared LTR-rich repeat architecture contains reproductive-group-associated divergence profiles, but those profiles do not account for the cellular association in the fitted models. Together these findings motivate an evolutionary hypothesis in which genome content, cellular organization, and organismal biology constrain one another without varying along a single proportional size axis. Adaptation, the direction of selection, and the balance of DNA gain and loss remain hypotheses rather than demonstrated mechanisms.

The manuscript's strongest contribution is this integrated within-genus comparison, not a claim to have first discovered genome-size variation, established a unique developmental cascade, or measured ectopic deletion rates. Figures 5–10 and 13 carry the cellular and causal-model argument; Figures 1–2 and 12 establish the repeat context and divergence pattern; Figure 11 explicitly tests the proposed connection. Figures 3–4 retain useful exploratory context. No figure was removed or modified during this review.
