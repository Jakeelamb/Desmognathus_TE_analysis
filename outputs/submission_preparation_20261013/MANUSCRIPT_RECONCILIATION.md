## Proposed title and study aim

A neutral association title preserves the topic without claiming causal direction or a demonstrated historical reduction in genome size:

> Associations among genome size, transposon communities, and erythrocyte architecture in Desmognathus salamanders

The following study-aim paragraph is ready for author review:

> Here, we examine associations among transposable-element communities, genome size, and erythrocyte architecture in Desmognathus salamanders. We characterize repeat composition and divergence profiles in 34 species and quantify selected erythrocyte and nuclear areas in 24 species. For those 24 species, we derive conditional genome-size estimates from nuclear integrated optical density, anchored to the D. fuscus assembly scale. Twenty-one species occur in both panels. We also compare an LTR terminal-to-internal mapping-depth proxy with genome size. Phylogenetic regressions and comparisons of specified candidate hypotheses evaluate cellular associations, their sensitivity to species maximum body size and animal weighting, and their persistence after adjustment for the relative low-divergence LTR signature. These observational analyses compare associations and compatible causal hypotheses while leaving causal direction and historical insertion and DNA-loss mechanisms unresolved.

Keep mutation-pressure, nucleotypic, and nucleus-cell mechanistic models as competing motivations in the Introduction. The literature can justify these hypotheses. It cannot establish causal orientation in this dataset. Consolidate the duplicate Introduction passages after verifying their primary citations.
