# What the expanded analyses support

The most defensible organizing result is coordinated genome and cellular variation, with a relatively consistent genome–cell association and more weakly determined placement of organismal body size. Repeat-divergence patterns provide a second axis of differentiation associated with reproductive strategy. The data do not currently establish that this repeat signature causes genome expansion or mediates cellular architecture.

## Sampling robustness (Figure 9)

Equal weighting across the sampled animals preserves the positive genome–cell association after SVL adjustment: standardized beta 0.583, 95% t interval 0.225–0.941. The cell–nucleus coefficient is 0.650 (0.267–1.032). The genome–nucleus coefficient conditional on cell area and SVL remains imprecise: 0.127 (−0.349–0.602).

The original four-model cell-bridge hypothesis ranks first in 216/250 hierarchical measurement resamples and 24/24 single-species deletions. Both focal slopes are positive in every resample. These counts establish stability within that limited candidate set and sampled measurement panel, not posterior probabilities of a causal mechanism. The 22-species equal-animal genome–cell interval is only narrowly above zero: 0.468 (0.004–0.933), P = 0.048.

Seven morphology species and four IOD species have a single animal. The two assays share 41 animals but only 180 exact object identifiers; the hierarchical analysis treats assays independently rather than inventing paired measurement covariance. Species medians describe selected upper-tail erythrocytes, not a random sample of all erythrocytes. No visual outliers were removed.

## Nuclear allocation (Figure 10)

The conditional log10 nucleus-area on log10 cell-area slope is 0.450 (0.170–0.730). Testing the slope against 1 gives P = 0.000504. The equal-animal mean-of-medians slope is 0.483 (0.257–0.710), and the central 95% range across 1,000 paired hierarchical refits is 0.306–0.633. Thus nucleus and cell areas covary, while proportional scaling is inconsistent with this fitted conditional model. Paired nuclear fractions supply a complementary observation, with equal-animal species summaries ranging from 13.64% to 19.12%.

This is not a structural allometric exponent corrected for predictor error. Cell-area error, the conditional regression axis, and the original selection of large cells can affect the slope. The manuscript can describe differentiated cellular and nuclear scaling in the sampled cells, while keeping mechanistic explanations as hypotheses.

## TE integration and divergence (Figures 11–12)

The focal TE endpoint is the fraction of LTR aligned-hit bases in divergence bins 0–4, divided by all LTR aligned-hit bases. It is a relative low-divergence representation, not absolute genomic occupancy, insertion rate, or a calibrated age. The all-hit fraction decomposes exactly into LTR representation among all aligned hits multiplied by the within-LTR low-divergence fraction.

Across the exact 21-species overlap, the TE score is not clearly associated with genome size after SVL adjustment: beta 0.311 (−0.381–1.003), P = 0.358. TE coefficients in the conditional cell and nucleus regressions are also imprecise. The genome–cell coefficient remains positive after adding TE: 0.527 (0.165–0.888). The conditional cell–nucleus coefficient changes little, but its interval narrowly crosses zero after adding TE: 0.633 (−0.004–1.270). The smaller panel and extra predictor therefore reduce precision.

Across all 34 TE species, reproductive strategy remains associated with the within-LTR fraction after phylogeny and SVL adjustment (approximate ML likelihood-ratio test, 2 df, P = 0.000568). The adjusted terrestrial-egg/aquatic-larva contrast versus aquatic eggs is 0.439 on the logit scale (0.061–0.818); the direct-development contrast is imprecise. This association survives changing the TE denominator, but is exploratory: strategy labels were supplied after ordination review, groups contain few independent evolutionary transitions, and the focal endpoint is one part of a repeat landscape.

A diagnostic association with the separately measured dnaPipeTE unresolved fraction (Spearman rho −0.452, P = 0.0072) remains relevant to interpretation. It is not a phylogenetically adjusted test or an additional primary finding. This prevents treating the divergence pattern as a clean measurement of insertion activity. The configured dnaPipeTE sample is 1.5 Gb; its denominators are distinct from the RepeatMasker aligned-hit summaries.

## Where body size enters (Figure 13)

Broadening the finite model set changes the relative weights. In full24, a cell-linked body-size structure receives weight 0.524 and a genome-linked body-size structure 0.384 (delta CICc 0.624). Both preserve the genome–cell–nucleus backbone. The original structure with SVL directly predicting all three traits receives weight 0.065 in this expanded set, while retaining acceptable global fit; its extra paths carry a complexity cost. Its earlier weight 0.944 remains a valid comparison within the original four candidates and should not be read as an absolute probability.

Without aeneus and wrighti, three simpler classes are near-tied, including a model with body size disconnected. Reversing the eligible independence-test response order changes the first-ranked 22-species class, but the same three remain competitive. The current evidence supports the cellular associations more consistently than it locates body size in the network.

The full24 best-ranked class permits four acyclic arrow orientations with the same skeleton and unshielded colliders. The diagrams show representative alternatives. These observational comparisons cannot identify whether cell size determines body size, body size determines cell size, or genome size acts upstream along a particular equivalent chain.
