# Independent review: phylogenetic comparison and causal inference

Reviewer remit: audit existing results only. I inspected Figures 6, 8, 9 and 13, their numerical exports, frozen S30–S37, and the original path, body-size, animal-resampling and body-role scripts. No models were rerun or figures changed. Paths below are relative to `outputs/manuscript_story_20260924_01`; `F8`, `F9` and `F13` mean `figures/figure_8/analysis`, `figures/figure_9/analysis` and `figures/figure_13/analysis`. S-number citations identify the corresponding `inputs/datasets/Supplementary_Data_Sxx_*.csv` file and named rows.

**Panel verdict.** Lead with a reproducible cellular association structure: across the sampled species, genome-size estimates covary with erythrocyte area, and erythrocyte area covaries with nuclear area; maximum body length alone does not explain these associations. The strongest causal hypothesis is that cellular size regulation connects DNA amount and nuclear dimensions. The evidence does not identify which variable changes first, establish complete mediation, or establish a TE-to-development causal sequence.

**The positive finding is substantive.** With SVL included, the standardized genome–cell coefficient is 0.545, with 95% t interval [0.192, 0.899], and the cell–nucleus coefficient is 0.683 [0.222, 1.144] (`F8/regression_coefficients.csv`, `full24`, `genome_to_cell_body_adjusted:g` and `cell_to_nucleus_body_adjusted:c`). Both remain positive under equal-animal summaries: 0.583 [0.225, 0.941] and 0.650 [0.267, 1.032] (`F9/point_coefficients.csv`, `animal_balanced/full24`, same relation/term identifiers). The hierarchical resamples retain positive estimates in every draw for these two coefficients (`F9/coefficient_sensitivity_summary.csv`, `hierarchical_bootstrap`). Thus the pattern survives meaningful adjustments to aggregation and organismal size; it is more than an appealing arrow diagram.

The absence claim is weaker. The genome coefficient in nuclear area conditioned on cell area and SVL is 0.051 [−0.499, 0.600] (`F8/regression_coefficients.csv`, `full24`, `genome_and_cell_to_nucleus_body_adjusted:g`). This establishes no detectable additional linear association at this precision, not a negligible direct effect. Neither a near-zero point estimate nor global-fit P=0.850 is an equivalence test. Calling the result “complete mediation by cell size” would exceed these data.

**What the classes actually constrain.** The three-trait cell-bridge class has weight 0.917; the nucleus-bridge class is 13.772 CICc units behind (S30, `primary`, corresponding model rows). The latter requires genome and cell size to be independent conditional on nucleus size. That claim is contradicted, P=0.000997 (S37, `primary`, `nucleus_bridge`, `c ~ n + g`). This is evidence against an *exclusive* nucleus-mediated chain for the measured traits under the fitted assumptions. It does not reject DNA→nucleus biology broadly or nuclear contributions within a richer network. In particular, measurement error in nuclear area could break this conditional independence even if the underlying biological chain were genome→true nuclear property→cell: conditioning on an imperfect mediator measurement does not fully condition on the mediator. Likewise, the SVL-only fork is contradicted, P=0.000238 (`F8/path_model_comparison.csv`, `body_adjusted_four/full24/body_only`): body size cannot simply replace the cellular relationships.

Three orientations of the genome–cell–nucleus chain encode the same conditional independence: genome-first, nucleus-first, and cell as their common parent. Figure 13 correctly enumerates four equivalent orientations for H5 and ten for H1 (`F13/equivalence_classes.csv`, `cell_body_branch`, `body_upstream_cell`). Identical skeletons and unshielded colliders cannot identify direction from conditional independences alone. This is a formal identification boundary, not merely low power ([Verma and Pearl, primary technical report](https://ftp.cs.ucla.edu/tech-report/1991-reports/910020.pdf)).

**The major objection concerns body-size causation.** Figure 8's 0.944 weight applies to four candidates that all place body size upstream. In the expanded set, that same model has weight 0.0646, while H5 and H7 have weights 0.524 and 0.384, separated by only 0.624 CICc (`F13/model_comparison.csv`, `full24/primary`, `body_upstream_cell`, `cell_body_branch`, `genome_body_branch`). H5 wins despite worse Fisher C than H1, 5.995 versus 0.326, because its parameter count is smaller, q=7 versus 9. The result favors a simpler compatible structure; it does not demonstrate that cells cause body length. H5, H7 and H8 all preserve the same cellular skeleton, so this uncertainty does not erase the cellular finding.

Excluding *aeneus* and *wrighti*, H8, H7 and H5 have weights 0.387, 0.295 and 0.283, all within 0.626 CICc; reversing the independence-test order makes H5 first (`F13/model_comparison.csv`, `subset22/primary` and `subset22/reverse_test_order`). Body-size placement is unresolved. These are also the two smallest species and the two direct developers (`F8/README.md`; `traits_joined.csv`). Their removal simultaneously changes size range, development and taxon composition. It cannot show whether development causes genome reduction, genome size constrains development, or a lineage-specific ecological process influences both. Maximum species SVL is not the body measurement of the microscopy animals.

**Robustness is real but bounded.** The animal-aware body-adjusted analysis ranks cell bridge first in 216/250 draws, with weight quantiles 0.118–0.973, and in all 24 single-species deletions (`F9/model_sensitivity_summary.csv`, `cell_bridge`). This is substantial stability with genuine residual model uncertainty. Figure 6's 246/250 first ranks concern a different three-trait comparison and resampling analysis (S32, `measurement_bootstrap_primary/cell_bridge`); neither statistic validates the expanded eight-class comparison, which has no measurement/tree bootstrap (`F13/METHODS.md`).

Tree stability needs the taxon qualification. All 200 published-tree fits rank cell bridge first, but all also leave nucleus bridge competitive (S32, `published_bootstrap_trees_18`). Even the focal tree restricted to those 18 species gives weights 0.434 and 0.322, ΔCICc=0.593 (`figures/figure_6/analysis/reduced_taxon_comparison.csv`, `focal_tree_18`). Taxon coverage, rather than alternative topology alone, materially affects discrimination. Simulation recovery is likewise asymmetric: true cell bridge ranks first in 78–80% of simulations, true nucleus bridge in 41–49%, and true genome bridge in 29–33% (S36, generating-class/regime rows). These simulations assess the specified generating models, not the truth probability of the empirical winner.

**Measurement and evolutionary interpretation.** Many key full24 residual λ estimates sit at the lower bound, whereas `g ~ b` has λ=1 (`F8/regression_coefficients.csv`). Residual λ describes the fitted regression errors; it neither establishes absence of trait phylogenetic signal nor identifies an evolutionary mechanism ([Revell 2010](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2041-210X.2010.00044.x)). Conditional intervals omit uncertainty in λ.

Seven morphology species and four IOD species have only one sampled animal. Across assays, 41 animals and 180 objects overlap, yet assays are independently resampled (`F9/verification.json`; `F9/README.md`). Thus cross-assay measurement covariance and unobserved between-animal variation remain unresolved. Within-species error can change comparative estimates, rather than merely enlarge uncertainty ([Ives, Midford and Garland 2007](https://doi.org/10.1080/10635150701313830)). The reviewed upper-tail cell sample further restricts the estimand. Shared segmentation, staining, or selection could contribute to covariation; its direction is not established. Common multiplicative uncertainty in the *fuscus* anchor alone cancels from centered log traits. Differential stain/batch effects do not generally cancel: the morphology README reports an unresolved standard-to-target run map and approximately 41% discrepancy between the two standard-slide median IOD values; the released denominator is the finalized *fuscus* target-species estimate, not a recovered run-matched standard (`/home/jake/Projects/Desmognathus_TE/analyses/02_morphology/README.md`, “Interpretation boundary”). This is a concrete calibration limitation, separate from common absolute-scale uncertainty.

**Competing explanation and manuscript priority.** Coordinated cellular physiology, unmeasured ecology or developmental history could generate the observed pattern without genome-first mediation; selection on cellular properties could also accompany changes in genome architecture over evolutionary time. These are competing hypotheses, not demonstrated replacements. TE and development are absent from these fitted path equations, so appending either to the arrows is unsupported. The analyses reuse the same species observations through multiple exploratory extensions; bootstrap draws and new candidate sets are not independent confirmations. The manuscript should foreground cellular integration, report contradictions of the exclusive nucleus-chain and body-only models for these measured traits, and present TE turnover, developmental association and causal direction as mechanistic questions motivating further work.
