## Central question and contribution

**Question:** How do repeat composition and sequence-divergence profiles vary within a salamander radiation, and which measured repeat and cellular properties covary with estimated genome size?

**Working title:** *Repeat landscapes and cellular scaling across Desmognathus salamanders*

**Central interpretation:** A broadly shared LTR-rich repeat repertoire contains structured differences in repeat dominance and sequence-divergence profiles. The divergence profiles distinguish sampled reproductive groups and lineages; genome-size comparisons suggest differences in TE evenness and show a positive association with selected erythrocyte area. The current LTR terminal-to-internal depth proxy provides no detectable explanation for genome-size variation.

The contribution is a comparative test across closely related species, with unusually clear contrasts in repeat-divergence representation and an accompanying cellular comparison. These observations motivate hypotheses about repeat accumulation and retention. They do not identify transposition rates, DNA-loss rates, or a causal developmental pathway.

## Draft manuscript synopsis

We compared transposable-element composition and sequence-divergence profiles across 34 species of Desmognathus and examined their relationship to estimated genome size and erythrocyte morphology in overlapping species panels. All species shared an LTR-rich repeat composition, while the relative representation of TE groups and their divergence profiles varied across lineages and reproductive strategies. Direct developers had the lowest representation of low-divergence LTR sequence, aquatic-egg species were intermediate, and terrestrial-egg species with aquatic larvae had the highest values in the current aligned-hit summaries. Among 21 species with overlapping measurements, TE evenness tended to decline with estimated genome size. Across 19 species, the LTR terminal-to-internal depth ratio showed no detectable genome-size association under either of two domain filters. Across 24 species, estimated genome size was positively associated with selected erythrocyte area, with a weaker association with nuclear area. These findings describe repeat differentiation within a shared TE repertoire and connect genome-size variation to cellular dimensions. Lineage and reproductive strategy remain intertwined in the repeat comparisons; the genome estimates are IOD derived, and the cellular measurements describe selected large erythrocytes.

This synopsis is a writing draft, not a claim that all inferred mechanisms have been tested. Exact model details and estimates belong in the Results, legends, and tables below.

## Results sequence

1. **A shared LTR-rich repeat composition spans the sampled radiation.** Establish all 34 TE species, the dominant repeat groups, reproductive strategies, and the available genome-size estimates. Explain what the repeat fractions measure once, at their first appearance.
2. **Repeat-divergence profiles differ across lineages and reproductive strategies.** Make current Figure 3 the central TE result. Describe individual-species variation alongside group summaries and retain the contrast among LTR, LINE, and TIR profiles. Use sequence divergence rather than dated insertion ages or measured activity.
3. **TE evenness tends to decline with estimated genome size.** Present the complete 21-species comparison before reproductive-strategy subsets. Interpret the fixed-top-ten indices as dominance/evenness among major superfamilies. Report Shannon and Gini–Simpson together; their P values of 0.053 and 0.035 describe a similar uncertain overall pattern. Do not select a metric or subgroup because it passes a significance threshold.
4. **The LTR terminal-to-internal depth proxy shows no detectable genome-size association.** Retain the distributions and the 19-species fit. The five-domain and four-domain slopes are both near zero. This bounds the explanatory value of the measured proxy without claiming that deletion is absent or equal among species.
5. **Estimated genome size covaries with selected erythrocyte area.** Present the three cellular associations together, with the genome–cell association as the clearest result. Introduce the conditional-association analysis briefly, with its detailed model comparison and sensitivities in the supplement.

The Discussion should connect these results through the question of how variation develops within a shared repeat repertoire. It should distinguish repeat composition, divergence representation, and cellular scaling, and should not draw untested arrows between them.

## Two focused computational refinements using existing data

These are proposals, not completed results or prerequisites for producing better figures.

1. **Clarify the principal divergence result.** Decompose the low-divergence statistic into the order's share of all aligned hits and the fraction of that order below 5% divergence. Retain the original all-hit statistic and the complete curves. A focused phylogenetic comparison can then evaluate the reproductive-strategy association conditional on the available tree, with effect intervals and explicit recognition of limited independent strategy transitions. Fix the questions and contrasts before inspecting new results; do not search thresholds for separation.
2. **Make the genome-size comparisons directly inspectable.** Export the existing diversity-model coefficients and intervals, then use a consistent influence and measurement-support audit for the focal diversity–genome and genome–cell relationships. Reuse existing cellular sensitivities. Retain all species in the primary figures; sensitivity restrictions must have measurement-based definitions and their results must be shown regardless of direction.

The already discussed LTR boundary check remains a separate computational validation using existing assemblies and depth files. Re-mapping feasibility depends on locating the original reads; the manuscript plan does not assume that those reads have been recovered or that new sequencing will occur.

## Proportional treatment of limitations

The main Methods should state the IOD-based genome-size construction, upper-tail morphology sampling, species-level joins, and assay denominators. A focused Discussion paragraph should explain calibration and limited animal support, alongside the post hoc and phylogenetic scope of the strategy comparisons. Exact diagnostics remain in supplementary tables. These limitations set the scope of inference; they do not require every result paragraph to repeat the entire audit.

Do not conclude that developmental mode causes TE changes, that the ratio measures an absolute deletion rate, that a null test proves equal rates, or that path-model arrows establish a mechanism. The observed comparative patterns and their effect estimates supply the argument.
