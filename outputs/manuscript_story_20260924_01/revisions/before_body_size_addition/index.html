Central question: Which causal hypotheses can explain the relationships among genome size, nuclear size, and cell size?

The 24-species phylogenetic path comparison favors the class in which cell size connects genome-size and nuclear-size variation. Figure 6 shows the competing models, the conditional associations behind that result, and the causal directions that remain compatible under these models. Figures 1–4 establish the repeat-evolution context; Figure 5 shows the pairwise cellular relationships.

The exact 380 primary LTR/Gypsy elements from 30 species are shown in the canonical phylogenetic row order. Gray horizontal outlines are log2-ratio densities with one pooled bandwidth, equal maximum widths, and tails trimmed to the observed range; violin widths do not represent sample size. Hollow points show all retained elements, jittered only vertically. Blue bars show released species medians of untransformed ratios; thin blue intervals are the released 95% intervals from 2,000 within-species element bootstraps. The right-hand labels give genome size (pg/1C) and element count; n/a denotes unavailable genome size without imputation. The depth ratio is length-weighted mean per-base depth across both terminal LTRs divided by mean depth across the entire intervening sequence, including zero-depth bases. The primary branch retains LTR/Gypsy elements at least 3,000 bp long with at least five correctly parsed TEsorter domains, inclusive within-species 1.5-IQR fences, and at least 80% positive-depth coverage in all three regions. The dotted ratio-one line denotes equal mapped depth; it is not an empirically calibrated no-deletion baseline. This proxy is not an ectopic-recombination or DNA-loss rate. Elements are not independent biological replicates. Nineteen species with both genome-size estimates and primary LTR ratios contribute one median each (239 elements in total). The existing stored Pagel-lambda PGLS fit, coefficient interval, and predictions are reused without refitting: log10 median ratio is regressed on log10 genome size. Stored genome coordinates and intervals in Gbp are divided by 0.978 to display pg/1C consistently with the other manuscript figures. This constant unit conversion leaves the log-log slope, its interval, P value, and all fitted ratio values unchanged. The slope is 0.024196 (95% t interval -0.560865 to 0.609257; P = 0.931489); lambda = 6.2e-09 at its lower boundary. The blue band is a coefficient interval for the fitted geometric mean, not an individual-species prediction interval. Gray bars show released genome-size intervals and element-bootstrap median intervals; these measurement uncertainties are not propagated through the regression. The interval permits associations in either direction; the nonsignificant test does not establish equivalence. Original source-tree branch lengths were retained in the stored fit; publication and calibration provenance remain unresolved. Genome sizes are IOD-derived estimates in pg/1C anchored to the D. fuscus assembly scale; they are not independently measured species C-values and their intervals exclude assembly-anchor uncertainty. This figure assembles existing results only; no filtering rules, data values, model estimates, or confidence intervals were changed. Phylogenetic path comparisons favor the class connecting genome size and nucleus area through cell area in the 24-species dataset. The winning cell-bridge equivalence class supports a mechanistic question about how the three traits covary, while retaining all three compatible causal orientations. (a) The cell-bridge class contains three distinct directed acyclic graphs, reproduced from the frozen candidate-DAG enumeration. Genome size -> cell area -> nucleus area, its reverse chain, and cell area -> both traits all imply genome–nucleus conditional independence given cell area. Arrow labels are direction-specific standardized phylogenetic regression coefficients from S28; every child in these diagrams has one parent, so its displayed coefficient comes from the matching two-variable regression. Arrow width is proportional to the absolute coefficient. Traits are log10-transformed and standardized to unit sample standard deviation across 24 species. The genome-first orientation has genome -> cell beta = 0.71666 and cell -> nucleus beta = 0.57930. Reverse and fork coefficients are retrieved separately, rather than copied or averaged; they agree after rounding to two decimals. These orientations are statistically indistinguishable in this three-trait comparison and share one class weight of 0.91674; their common conditional structure is the supported result. The highlighted orientation is a hypothesis, not a uniquely identified causal direction. (b) Ten testable path-equivalence classes are ranked by CICc using 24 species, standardized log10 traits, the supplied dated tree and Pagel-lambda regressions. The cell-bridge class has CICc = 13.3829, Fisher C = 0.04959, one independence claim, global-fit P = 0.97551, and relative weight = 0.91674. Its tested claim is the residual genome–nucleus association after conditioning on cell area (P = 0.97551). All nine other tested classes fail the P >= 0.05 global-fit criterion; the nucleus-bridge class has delta CICc = 13.77171 and global-fit P = 0.0009971. Weights compare only the supplied ten classes; six saturated DAGs impose no testable d-separation claim and are not ranked. All primary component regressions have fitted lambda approximately 1e-7. The class weight measures relative model support and is distinct from the direction-specific path coefficients in panel a. (c) Upper rows show the two component regressions in the genome-first hypothesis: genome -> cell beta = 0.71666 (95% normal interval 0.42523 to 1.00810), and cell -> nucleus beta = 0.57930 (0.23869 to 0.91992). Lower rows show separate conditional regressions: genome -> nucleus adjusted for cell area, beta = 0.00792 (-0.49195 to 0.50780); genome -> cell adjusted for nucleus area, beta = 0.57486 (0.27994 to 0.86977). Circles identify displayed single-parent paths and triangles identify conditional comparisons. All four fits use 24 species, standardized log10 traits and Pagel-lambda phylogenetic regression. Intervals are normal approximations from S28 (beta +/- 1.96 SE), not bootstrap intervals or estimates of indirect effects. The broad conditional genome–nucleus interval does not establish an exactly zero effect. (d) Cell bridge ranks first in 246/250 measurement resamples, 24/24 leave-one-species-out fits, 4/4 evolutionary-model specifications and 200/200 published-tree fits. The first and third checks retain 24 species, species omission retains 23 per fit, and the published-tree analysis contains only 18 species. All 200 published-tree fits also retain nucleus bridge as globally admissible and within delta CICc <= 2, so top ranking does not indicate unique support. On the focal tree pruned to the same 18 species, cell-bridge weight is 0.43357 and nucleus-bridge weight is 0.32240 (delta CICc = 0.59252); the published main tree gives essentially the same values. These reduced-species results primarily expose dependence of discrimination on taxon coverage, rather than establishing strong topology robustness. Accompanying figures retain all eight simulation-recovery settings and the original marginal-versus-conditional coefficient comparison. All results are taken from the frozen path-analysis release; this figure introduces no model refitting or data filtering. Genome size is an IOD-derived D. fuscus assembly-anchored estimate, and cellular traits describe selected large erythrocytes and their nuclei. Measurement uncertainty and taxon coverage are evaluated by the displayed sensitivity analyses.

Reference-style candidate depicting direct and indirect paths under a specified genome-first causal hypothesis: genome -> cell, cell -> nucleus and genome -> nucleus. Component equations are cell ~ genome and nucleus ~ genome + cell, fitted with Pagel-lambda phylogenetic regressions on log10-transformed, sample-standardized traits from 24 species.
