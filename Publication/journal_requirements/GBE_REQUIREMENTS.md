# Genome Biology and Evolution publication requirements

**Target journal:** *Genome Biology and Evolution* (GBE; Oxford University Press for the Society for Molecular Biology and Evolution)
**Accessed:** 2026-07-13
**Scope of this report:** Current requirements relevant to the planned *Desmognathus* comparative-genomics, repeat-landscape, microscopy, and phylogenetic analyses. Only first-party GBE/OUP sources are used.

## Bottom line for this manuscript

The appropriate submission category is most likely an **Article**, not a Methods paper or Genome Resource. GBE defines Articles as analyses of genomic data or methods/software that produce new genome-scale insight. The planned paper combines comparative TE, ectopic-recombination, cell/nucleus/genome-size, and phylogenetic analyses. GBE specifically says that Genome Resource manuscripts with extensive evolutionary analyses should instead be submitted as Articles or Letters. An Article allows up to **10,000 words of text excluding references**, **8 total tables plus figures**, **100 references**, a **250-word abstract**, and a **150-word significance statement**. This classification remains an editorial decision. [GBE Instructions to Authors: Types of Manuscripts](https://academic.oup.com/gbe/pages/general_instructions)

For an Article, use this section order:

1. Abstract
2. Significance statement
3. Introduction
4. Results
5. Discussion
6. Materials and Methods
7. Data Availability

Therefore, the complete methods can be drafted in DOCX, but the **Materials and Methods section belongs after the Discussion in the submission manuscript**. Supplementary methods, tables, figures, and their legends must be uploaded as supplementary files rather than inserted into the main manuscript. [GBE Instructions to Authors: Articles](https://academic.oup.com/gbe/pages/general_instructions)

## GBE-specific manuscript requirements

These are explicit journal requirements, not inferred house style.

### Initial submission

- Prepare a **single main-manuscript PDF** with main-text tables and figures inline, page numbers, and continuous line numbers.
- Use double spacing, defined by GBE as at least 6 mm between lines, with 25 mm (1 inch) margins on every side.
- Avoid footnotes and embed all fonts when producing the PDF.
- The online-submission section asks for three PDF files:
  1. cover letter;
  2. manuscript, including the significance statement, Materials and Methods after the Discussion, tables/figures, continuous line numbers, and Data Availability statement; and
  3. one supplementary-information PDF containing supplementary text, tables, and figures and fitting a standard page.
- Do not place supplementary figures or files in the main-manuscript PDF.
- At revision, submit text, tables, and figures as separate editable/publication-quality files.

Source: [GBE Instructions to Authors: Preparation and Online Submission](https://academic.oup.com/gbe/pages/general_instructions).

### Article front and end matter

- Title: no more than 150 characters including spaces.
- Abstract: no more than 250 words for an Article; avoid reference citations.
- Keywords: up to six, below the abstract; they should not merely repeat title words.
- Significance statement: no more than 150 words and ordinarily 2–3 sentences; it should state prior knowledge/gap, key findings, and why those findings address the gap in language accessible to a science undergraduate.
- Data Availability: required in the end matter under the heading **Data availability**.
- Figure legends: included at the end of the manuscript text.
- Main-article alt text: required for every image, figure, illustration, and photograph. Put it immediately below the corresponding legend and begin with **Alt text:**.
- Field-collected samples: GBE expects compliance with Nagoya Protocol principles and invites an appropriate statement in Materials and Methods or Acknowledgements.
- Animal work: state whether applicable institutional and national care-and-use guidelines were followed.

Source: [GBE Instructions to Authors: Sections, Accessibility, and Other Information](https://academic.oup.com/gbe/pages/general_instructions).

## Figures

### Explicit GBE requirements

| Item | GBE requirement |
|---|---|
| Initial submission | Main figures inline in the single manuscript PDF. |
| Revised submission | Each figure on a separate page/file, at approximately final magnification. |
| Accepted revision formats | `.jpg`, `.gif`, `.tif`, `.pdf`, or `.eps`. |
| Figure font | Sans serif, such as **Arial or Helvetica**. Use a uniform font size within each figure. |
| Minimum label size | **Never smaller than 6 pt at final magnification.** |
| Color figures and photographs | At least **300 pixels per inch at final printed size**. |
| Black-and-white line drawings | At least **600 pixels per inch at final printed size**. |
| Color space | GBE explicitly requires **CMYK rather than RGB** for color art at revision/publication quality. |
| Color publication | Color submitted to GBE is published online in color at no author charge. |
| Citation | Refer to illustrations as “Figure 1” in text and as “Fig. 1.” only in the legend. |
| Legend | First sentence should state the key insight; subsequent sentences give detail. Define symbols and abbreviations. Legends go at the end of the manuscript. |
| Alt text | Required for every main-article figure/image and placed directly below the legend, preceded by “Alt text:”. |

Source: [GBE Instructions to Authors: Illustrations, Electronic Submission, Legends, and Accessibility](https://academic.oup.com/gbe/pages/general_instructions).

### Not specified by GBE

GBE's current instructions do **not** specify any of the following for ordinary figures:

- an exact single-column or double-column figure width;
- a maximum figure height;
- a required ggplot theme;
- a required panel-label style or size;
- a required ordinary-figure title size;
- an exact line weight;
- a maximum number of panels per figure;
- a specific color palette;
- a required manuscript-body font for a Word document.

GBE lists a page type area of 234 mm high × 185 mm double-column width or 89 mm single-column width **for revised tables**. Treating 89 mm and 185 mm as figure-width targets would be a practical inference, not an explicit GBE figure rule.

### General OUP artwork guidance — recommendations, not GBE rules

OUP labels its short artwork guidance as “tips rather than strict rules” and says journal-specific instructions take precedence. Relevant recommendations are:

- Prefer uncompressed TIF for raster images and EPS/SVG/PDF with embedded fonts for vector images. Because GBE does not list SVG among its revision formats, **PDF or EPS is the safer vector output for GBE**.
- Keep text at least 7 pt, line thickness between 0.25 and 1 pt, and fonts/colors/textures consistent.
- OUP's generic resolution guidance is 300 dpi for color halftones, 600 dpi for grayscale halftones, 600–900 dpi for combined halftone/line art, and 1,200 dpi for monochrome line art. Pure vector output has no intrinsic dpi.
- Do not upscale raster files to manufacture dpi.
- Avoid pale colors and red–green distinctions; do not use color as the only carrier of meaning.
- Supply multipanel figures as one file; put captions in the manuscript rather than inside the image file.

Source: [OUP journals—guidance for preparing artwork (PDF)](https://static.primary.prod.gcms.the-infra.com/static/site/journals/document/images-author-guidance.pdf?node=1bf05d0b2fbd9c529a23&version=490455%3A30c2211aa70bba63a5ee).

OUP's accessibility guide further recommends, where feasible:

- 12–14 pt text in figures;
- sans-serif fonts;
- at least 3:1 contrast for graphical objects, 4.5:1 for normal text, and 3:1 for large text;
- non-color cues such as direct labels, point shapes, patterns, or line types.

These are accessibility recommendations, not GBE's technical minimum. The practical hierarchy is: **use Arial/Helvetica to satisfy GBE; never go below 6 pt; prefer at least 7 pt to meet OUP artwork guidance; make text larger when figure dimensions permit**. [OUP Making Figures Accessible, Journals Edition (PDF)](https://static.primary.prod.gcms.the-infra.com/static/umbrella/document/Figures_accessibility_journals_edition_v2.2_2024.pdf?node=aef6670590405d43ef10).

### Reproducible ggplot house specification for this project

The following is a conservative project convention, **not a claim that GBE explicitly mandates these exact settings**:

- Arial throughout; fall back to Helvetica only if Arial cannot be embedded.
- Export analysis plots as vector PDF with embedded fonts and as CMYK 300-dpi TIF only when a raster submission file is needed.
- Build at final intended width. Use 89 mm for single-column candidates and 185 mm for double-column candidates as working targets, while recognizing those widths are inferred from GBE's listed table type area.
- Use at least 7–8 pt text at final size; use larger labels when space permits.
- Keep essential strokes within OUP's 0.25–1 pt recommendation and use 0.5 pt as a consistent default where legible.
- Use a color-vision-deficiency-safe palette, black text on a white background, and redundant shape/line-type/direct-label encodings.
- Do not include a plot title inside the figure unless needed for interpretation; let the manuscript legend carry the title-like statement.
- Give every figure a standalone legend and alt text.

### Optional graphical abstract

If prepared, GBE explicitly requires landscape orientation, 4:3 aspect ratio, at least 100 × 75 mm (or 4 × 3 in), TIF/EPS/editable PDF, 300–600 dpi minimum, and a sans-serif font such as Arial at 12–16 pt. It must be a new, unique summary graphic and uploaded separately with a clear filename such as `graphical_abstract.tiff`. [GBE Instructions to Authors: Graphical Abstract](https://academic.oup.com/gbe/pages/general_instructions).

## Supplementary information and finalized datasets

### Explicit GBE requirements

- All supplementary information—including supplementary tables and supplementary figure legends—must be uploaded separately as supplementary files, not placed in the main manuscript.
- At initial submission, GBE asks for a **single supplementary-information PDF** containing text, tables, and figures that fit a standard page.
- Large supplementary files should be placed in a public repository, ideally one assigning a DOI, and the manuscript Data Availability statement should point to it.
- Every underlying dataset should be public where ethically possible, either in the main manuscript, supporting files, or a public repository.
- New sequence data must be deposited in GenBank/DDBJ/EMBL.
- Sequence alignments used in the study must be supplied as supplementary data or deposited in a repository such as Figshare or Dryad.
- Code generated for the manuscript should be deposited on a persistent site such as Zenodo.
- Public datasets must be cited in the reference list using a persistent accession/identifier. GBE asks for Author(s), Year, Title, Publisher/repository, and Identifier, and demonstrates a citation beginning with the tag `[dataset]`.

Source: [GBE Instructions to Authors: Availability of Data and Materials](https://academic.oup.com/gbe/pages/general_instructions).

### General OUP supplementary-file guidance — subordinate to GBE

OUP's publisher-wide guidance says supplementary material should enhance but not be necessary to understand the article; be separate, cited in the manuscript, self-explanatorily named, styled consistently with the manuscript, browser-compatible, and no larger than 2 MB per file. It will not be copyedited. OUP does not permit unstable hosting such as personal/institutional websites, Google Docs, or YouTube; use OUP hosting or an accepted repository. [OUP Preparing and Submitting Your Manuscript: Supplementary Material](https://academic.oup.com/pages/for-authors/journals/preparing-and-submitting-your-manuscript).

Because the planned audited CSV release is likely larger and more numerous than a single compact supplementary PDF, the cleanest compliant approach is:

1. Put narrative supplementary methods, figure legends, supplementary figures, and compact tables into `Supplementary_Information.pdf`.
2. Deposit finalized CSV datasets, code, tree files, alignments, and large microscopy audit artifacts in a versioned DOI-issuing repository.
3. Cite the repository dataset in the reference list and give its DOI in Data Availability.
4. Include a compact dataset index in the supplementary PDF that maps each analysis to its deposited file(s).

GBE does not prescribe a CSV naming convention. A defensible local convention is `Supplementary_Data_S01_<short_description>.csv`, plus a machine-readable manifest recording filename, description, analysis, row count, column count, SHA-256 checksum, source/provenance, and software/script version. This convention is a project recommendation, not a journal requirement.

## Data and code availability checklist for this study

GBE applies a requirement equivalent to OUP's strongest non-peer-reviewed-data tier: public release of underlying data is a condition of publication where ethically possible, and a Data Availability statement is mandatory. [GBE Instructions to Authors](https://academic.oup.com/gbe/pages/general_instructions); [OUP Research Data](https://academic.oup.com/pages/open-research/research-data).

For this project, the release should include or persistently identify:

- genome assembly accessions and acquisition metadata;
- de novo TE libraries and their provenance;
- RepeatMasker/landscape summaries underlying every reported panel;
- ectopic-recombination/LTR input and derived tables;
- cell and nucleus measurements used in species summaries;
- frozen mask-review decisions and the exact selected top-50 panels;
- nuclear IOD measurements, image-level QC fields, frozen inclusion decisions, calibration constant, and calibrated genome-size estimates;
- phylogenetic trees and exact trimmed trees used for each analysis;
- PGLS and path-analysis input tables and model-result tables;
- scripts or a versioned source snapshot needed to regenerate the tables and figures;
- a manifest linking every main/supplementary figure and reported statistic to its input dataset and analysis script.

The Data Availability statement should provide direct access through DOI(s) and/or accession numbers rather than only saying data are “available on request.” GBE explicitly requires public release where ethically possible.

## Open access, preprints, and charges

- GBE is fully open access and requires an open-access publication charge. The Article APC is generated dynamically from article type and author circumstances; the static instructions page does not provide a reliable fixed Article amount, so verify it in the linked calculator immediately before submission.
- SMBE members are eligible for a 20% membership discount, subject to proof.
- Read and Publish agreements may cover eligible corresponding authors. The qualifying institution must be the corresponding author's primary affiliation at submission; changing the corresponding author after submission to obtain coverage is not permitted.
- OUP offers automatic waivers/discounts for qualifying low- and middle-income-country corresponding authors. GBE also considers other waiver applications case by case.
- GBE does not levy page charges, but extensive proof-stage changes may be charged.
- Posting an Author's Original Version/preprint does not prevent submission. If the paper is accepted, authors must update the preprint status with the published DOI/link. GBE supports direct transfer from bioRxiv.

Source: [GBE Instructions to Authors: Publication Charges, Open Access, and Preprint Policy](https://academic.oup.com/gbe/pages/general_instructions).
