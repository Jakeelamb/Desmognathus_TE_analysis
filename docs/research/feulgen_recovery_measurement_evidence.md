# Feulgen nuclear IOD recovery: measurement evidence and pilot protocol

**Research date:** 2026-10-13. **Repository baseline:** `8a5c00b33c0359b4a47f2df2ead145039554d623`. This is a research and proposed-pilot note. No microscopy analysis, mask editing, environment installation, or release regeneration was performed for it. The frozen measurements remain historical evidence.

**Conclusion:** a recovery pilot is scientifically justified, but improved appearance, lower dispersion, or closer agreement between two standard slides cannot establish recovered DNA measurements. Start with original-pixel provenance and blinded nuclear-boundary review, then evaluate calibrated transmitted-light measurement and same-nucleus reimaging. A defensible absolute scale also needs independent reference DNA content and known nuclear DNA class. The relevant primary experiments show morphology-dependent optical errors and calibration-based remedies, rather than a universal image-cleanup remedy. [Bedi and Goldstein, 1976](https://doi.org/10.1083/jcb.71.1.68), [Puech and Giroud, 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10331622/).

## Local evidence and the questions it leaves

The supplied pilot snapshot contains 47 original nuclei: Process 413/specimen 32469 has 31; Process 414/specimen 32470 has 16. All remain unreviewed. The historical paired strict pools contain 9 and 6 respectively; 18 identities lack cell linkage. The reported unreviewed median discrepancy increases from approximately 41% to 65% after the existing filter. These are task-context observations, not fresh calculations in this note; retain the producer receipts and the definition of the percentage before comparing later results. The maintained [standard-review contract](../STANDARD_QC.md) preserves all identities and distinguishes technical assignment from manual acceptance.

The author identifies standards and unknowns as belonging to one staining run, acquired at 40× brightfield without oil using green-channel images and the same fixation protocol. These statements constrain the pilot; they do not establish achieved hydrolysis, camera response, historical mounting conditions, or equal exposure/storage histories. The [existing anchor audit](desmognathus_fuscus_genome_size_anchor.md) names `16.1 Gbp / 0.978` as an assembly-derived conditional 1C anchor, not an independently measured C-value of either standard animal.

A missing cell link is a failure of paired cell–nucleus morphology evidence. It need not exclude a nucleus-only photometry audit when nuclear identity, complete boundary, isolation, and source pixels can be verified. Conversely, successful cell linkage does not establish photometric validity. A normalized boundary-gradient/contrast score is a technical feature, not a calibrated axial-focus measurement. Template mismatch therefore leaves several explanations open: different morphology, boundary placement, sampling, contrast, refraction, or defocus. This separation is an inference from the workflow's measurement domains, not a new acceptance decision.

## What the underlying sources establish

The following summaries use primary experimental reports, the requested laboratory methods paper, and official instrument/measurement guidance. Numerical recommendations retain their original setting. They are not universal thresholds for salamander erythrocytes.

| Source, date, and setting | Direct evidence and applicability |
|---|---|
| [Hardie, Gregory and Hebert, 2002; pp. 738, 741–745](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/002215540205000601) | This review also reports the authors' vertebrate-nucleus experiments. Green-channel measurement targets the magenta Feulgen absorption peak; IOD uses clear-slide incident intensity and all pixels within an outlined nucleus. Their usual preference is 100× oil, but 40× is explicitly discussed as usable with reduced sampling precision; large nuclei are an exception to the high-magnification preference. Refractive-index liquid beneath the coverslip and objective immersion oil are distinct. They caution against generic image “background correction,” while requiring incident-light estimation. Their benign-overselection advice assumes non-nuclear pixels contribute no absorbance relative to that reference. Same-run, same-cell-type standards and controlled field density matter. Their trimming suggestion is not adopted here: selecting on the outcome would obscure the recovery problem. |
| [Smeulders and ten Kate, 1987](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20490051/) | Their low-resolution TV microscopy analysis addresses image formation, focus, diffraction, glare, sampling, noise, and computational error in Feulgen-stained cells. Approximately 5% population CV was attainable with adequate shading correction. That result establishes that low-resolution imaging can be useful under characterized conditions; it neither validates this archive nor fixes a permissible defocus threshold. The available evidence inspected here is the authors' abstract. |
| [Bedi and Goldstein, 1976](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/61968/) | Human leukocytes, squames, and sperm showed apparent DNA differences and erroneous diploid:haploid ratios before correction of glare/distribution errors. Corrected ratios approached two. Compact nuclei and nuclei with high perimeter-to-area ratios were affected differently. Some strongly stained sperm exceeded the instrument's absorbance range. These observations support testing morphology-dependent optical bias and dynamic range before interpreting standard disagreement as biology. They do not establish the magnitude or sign of error in these salamander images. |
| [Kindermann and Hilgers, 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8167099/) | The authors relocated identical rat hepatocyte and lymphocyte nuclei under different microscope settings. Their glare correction was calibrated from opaque bars on a micrometric glass, followed by background normalization; within-class CVs below 3% were reported. This is evidence for an instrument-specific physical correction. An arbitrary subtraction value learned from the two disagreeing standard slides is not that correction. Abstract-level evidence was inspected. |
| [Puech and Giroud, 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10331622/) | Their PRESS reference-slide work develops separate checks for temporal instability, nonlinearity, shading, glare, and segmentation efficiency. Passing one check does not qualify the other mechanisms. The clinical system qualification limits cannot be assumed satisfied retrospectively by a sharp-looking image or a low nucleus CV. Abstract and publisher-indexed first-page text were inspected; no unavailable numerical limit is imported into this pilot. |
| [ESACP consensus, 1995, sections IV–VIII](https://www.classimed.de/esacimag.html) | The official author-hosted reproduction asks for instrument stability/linearity/shading/glare checks, in-focus nuclei, visual segmentation control, identically prepared and same-bath references, and unchanged acquisition settings. Its performance examples include one diploid nucleus at 30 field sites with CV below 3%, a nondividing diploid population across sites below 5%, reference-population CV at most 6%, and IOD linearity checks using 2c/4c/8c classes. These are clinical cytometry targets with specific test designs, not automatic rules for this 47-nucleus archive. |
| [ESACP fourth update, 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11904464/) | The report explicitly makes additions to, rather than replacing, the 1997 consensus. It focuses on terminology and diagnostic interpretation. Its publication date is 2001; later web crawl/upload dates do not turn it into a recent experimental validation. Only the abstract was accessible in this pass, so numerical thresholds above are attributed to the inspected 1995 text, not to the inaccessible 2001 full text. |
| [EMVA 1288, Release 4.0 Linear, 16 June 2021; sections 6.5 and 6.9](https://www.emva.org/wp-content/uploads/EMVA1288Linear_4.0Release.pdf) | Official camera guidance separates linear-response characterization from the General module for broader camera classes. For characterization it specifies thermal equilibrium, high bit depth, documented gain/offset, paired illuminated and dark frames per exposure, and ordinarily at least 50 exposure levels; nine are permitted for production measurements. Linearity analysis uses dark-subtracted signal over 5–95% of saturation. These methods motivate the pilot's response test; a short microscopy exposure sweep is not a full EMVA certification. The [official release page](https://www.emva.org/standards-technology/emva-1288/emva-standard-1288-downloads-2/) identifies Release 4.0 as effective from 2021-06-21. |
| [Nikon official immersion-oil guidance; inspected 2026-10-13](https://www.microscope.healthcare.nikon.com/en_AOM/products/accessories/immersion-oil) | Objective immersion oil replaces the air gap between cover glass and an immersion objective; refractive-index/dispersion mismatch impairs performance. This clarifies which optical interface the oil changes. A dry objective should be used according to its design; adding oil to it is not a generic rescue experiment. The existing objective model, NA, cover-glass correction and mounting medium still need identification. |
| [Dewse and Potter, 1975](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/54953/) | Serial microspectrophotometry of Feulgen-stained human fibroblast nuclei found fading that depended on mountant and illumination history. Fading was faster in light, particularly during measurement illumination, than in darkness. This supports recording storage, mounting, and cumulative exposure, and minimizing illumination during repeated imaging. It does not provide a retrospectively valid fade correction for these slides. Abstract-level evidence was inspected. |
| [Greilhuber and Temsch, 2001](https://hrcak.srce.hr/en/160855) | Original experiments mainly used plant root meristems. Formaldehyde concentration/time/temperature, hydrolysis molarity/time/temperature, and post-hydrolysis washing influenced nuclear dye content; co-fixation of test and standard was advised for formaldehyde. Dark slide storage was relatively insensitive in their protocol. Their material, chemistry, and tested durations differ from the fibroblast fading study and this animal archive. These reports therefore do not establish a contradiction or a universal slide shelf life. Their exact plant hydrolysis/storage conditions must not be transplanted to the animal protocol. The full abstract was inspected; the linked PDF was inaccessible. |
| [Noureddine et al.; published online 2025-12-21, Sensors 2026, 26:57; sections 2.3–2.4 and Table 1](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26010057) | The inspected [publisher PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/sensors/sensors-26-00057/article_deploy/sensors-26-00057.pdf) uses Thionin, 16 wavelengths, calibrated OD images, and an empirical absorption/refraction decomposition. Across four slides, CV ranges changed from 5.1–6.7% to 4.2–6.6%, and 4c/2c ratios from 2.06–2.16 to 2.01–2.06. An RGB illustration is also shown, but the quantified ploidy comparison uses hyperspectral data versus averaged 600-nm images. The refraction component is explicitly not universal. Quantitative transport to green-only magenta-Feulgen salamander images is unsupported. The reported statistical test unit is insufficiently explicit for independent reconstruction, so its p-values are not used as acceptance evidence here. |
| [Ruifrok and Johnston, 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11531144/) | Their original color-deconvolution study uses stain-specific RGB absorption and tests DAB/hematoxylin/eosin mixtures, with quantification conditional on absence of bleaching and saturation. It supports the need for measured spectral components, not the transfer of standard histology vectors to Feulgen. It neither validates single-green-channel refraction removal nor restores information absent from the acquisition. Abstract-level evidence was inspected. |
| [Doležel et al., 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12541287/), [complete letter](https://biotechcenter.web.illinois.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Dolezel-et-al.-2002.pdf) | This primary correction letter derives approximately 0.978 × 10^9 base pairs per picogram from nucleotide-pair molecular mass and corrects a serious conversion/standard-value error. Conversion is a unit relationship, not verification of a DNA standard's species, ploidy, or true genome content. The PDF's filename says 2002; its journal publication and DOI record are 2003. |
| [OME/Bio-Formats conversion documentation, stable 8.5.0 at inspection](https://bio-formats.readthedocs.io/en/stable/users/comlinetools/conversion.html) | Official documentation distinguishes source series/channel/Z/time indices, explicit cropping, subresolutions/pyramids, compression, and undefined-pixel fill values. TIFF output is uncompressed by default; JPEG's maximum setting is described as nearly lossless. A lossless output container cannot undo loss in the original acquisition. This is an extraction specification, not a claim that the repository uses Bio-Formats 8.5.0 or that any current conversion is validated. |
| [Bland and Altman, 1986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2868172/) | The original method-comparison paper explains why correlation is insufficient to establish agreement and relates measurement differences to repeatability. Comparing matched differences is appropriate for a proposed same-nucleus comparison; a high correlation or a visually similar rank order is not an accuracy gate. Abstract-level evidence was inspected. |
| [Bland and Altman, 2007; author-posted full paper](https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~mb55/meas/bland2007.pdf) | Repeated observations on one individual are clustered. Treating each pair as independent can produce incorrect, overly narrow agreement limits; averaging repeats instead estimates agreement for averages unless within-unit error is handled. Here the corresponding units are nuclei nested in fields and slides. Extra camera frames are technical replication, not extra biological standards. |
| [Bland's correction to the 1986 repeated-measurements section; updated 2009-07-03](https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~mb55/meas/repeated.htm) | The author corrects the variance formula for comparing means of two replicates: the repeat-difference variance contributions use one-half, rather than the original one-quarter. This revision matters if that older formula is implemented. The pilot should use the later repeated-measurements treatment rather than silently copying the original formula. |
| [NIST TN 1297, Appendix A](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-appendix-law-propagation-uncertainty) | Official uncertainty guidance models the complete measurement process, including corrections, observers, instruments, samples and times, and propagates covariances as well as variances. It supports an uncertainty budget extending beyond nucleus-to-nucleus sampling error. It cannot assign a missing standard C-value uncertainty or quantify unobserved historical staining bias. |

The background recommendations are not a blanket contradiction. [Hardie](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/002215540205000601) uses one incident-light estimate for a demonstrably homogeneous field; [ESACP](https://www.classimed.de/esacimag.html) allows shading/local-background/glare correction. Those address calibrated acquisition effects. Neither establishes that arbitrary subtraction, rolling-ball removal, histogram normalization, or display stretching preserves this archive's absorbance. Their algorithm and assumptions must be specified before comparison.

## Photometric consequences that can be tested

These are algebraic deductions and proposed diagnostics, not newly measured effects in the archive. Let `p` denote a source pixel, `I(p)` linear transmitted-light signal, `I0(p)` incident-light signal, `D(p)` matched detector dark offset, `M` a nuclear mask, and `Apx` the physical pixel area. A calibrated model is:

```text
T(p) = [I(p) − D(p)] / [I0(p) − D(p)]
OD(p) = −log10(T(p))
physical absorbance integral = Apx × Σ[p in M] OD(p)
```

The model needs positive numerator/denominator, a characterized response, and an identifiable reference. Offsets and flat fields must correspond to the same settings and optical path. Pixels brighter than the reference can produce negative OD because of noise, refraction, or an inappropriate reference. Automatically zeroing them changes the estimator and must be a separately labeled sensitivity, not a silent cleanup. Instrument-calibrated glare subtraction and background normalization were experimentally tested by [Kindermann and Hilgers](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8167099/); this proposed formula does not substitute for their calibration procedure.

The supplied frozen standard-audit implementation uses a background of 255:

```text
IOD_frozen = Σ[p in M] log10(255 / max(uint8_pixel(p), 1))
```

This expression applies to the two designated standard images. The same-species
target pilot has recorded backgrounds of 255, 218 and 216. Its historical replay
must use each image's recorded background; a shared 255 reference is a separate
sensitivity calculation.

For an unclipped, zero-offset, linear image with a constant clear-slide reference `B`, using the same pixels and mask gives exactly:

```text
IOD_frozen − IOD_B = Nmask × log10(255 / B)
```

Thus `B < 255` adds a mask-area-dependent term. For illustration only, at `B = 190` the offset is approximately `0.128 OD` per pixel, or 128 summed units per 1,000 pixels. This is not an estimate for either standard. A ring of genuine blank-slide pixels contributes approximately zero to `IOD_B`, but contributes this positive offset to the frozen expression. Boundary sensitivity and light-reference sensitivity therefore need separate reporting. With a spatial reference, the offset is the sum of `log10(255 / B(p))` over the mask. The derivation fails where digitization, clipping, offsets, or nonlinear response invalidate the assumed intensities.

Three further deductions prevent misleading recovery claims:

1. **Shared gamma can sometimes cancel.** If the entire response is exactly `y = k × I^a`, an incident-referenced OD calculated from `y` equals `a` times true OD. A common exponent can cancel in a target/standard IOD ratio. This narrow result does not apply automatically to per-image processing, black offsets, clipping, piecewise tone curves, or a fixed 255 reference. Gamma metadata alone cannot establish the required model; measure the response.
2. **Different sampling changes a raw sum.** A finer pixel grid represents the same physical nucleus with more pixels. Compare physical area integrals or standard-normalized ratios within each acquisition condition when changing objective/binning/resolution. Comparing raw pixel sums at 40× and 100× would confound sampling with absorption.
3. **Capturing every blurred pixel does not guarantee preserved IOD.** In an idealized linear-intensity image blurred by a positive normalized kernel `K`, convexity gives `−log(K*T) ≤ K*(−log T)`. Over complete support, integrated OD can decrease even when integrated intensity is conserved. Actual brightfield refraction need not obey this simple blur model. The deduction shows why extending a mask or sharpening an image is not proof of recovered absorption.

For nuclear DNA-class factors `q_target` and `q_reference` expressed in haploid-genome equivalents, unit accounting gives `C_target_1C = (Q_target / Q_reference) × (q_reference / q_target) × C_reference_1C`, where `Q` is comparable integrated absorbance. Inferring `q` from the same disputed IOD values would make this accounting circular; it needs independent biological evidence.

The clipping floor maps recorded zeros to the same finite value, approximately `log10(255) = 2.40654`, as recorded ones. Their original transmitted intensity is unidentified. Quantization and already-clipped signals cannot be inverted uniquely. Check exact minimum/maximum counts on native nuclear and reference pixels, with missing/padded pixels identified separately.

## Proposed staged pilot

This is an original test design synthesized from the evidence above. It is not a protocol already accepted or executed on these samples. Advance only when the preceding stage produces interpretable evidence. Save each new diagnostic in a fresh scratch destination and preserve immutable raw images, masks, coordinates, measurements, and source checksums.

| Priority and test | Concrete design and decision it informs |
|---|---|
| **1. Native-pixel extraction audit** | Identify original acquisition files and inspect dimensions, bit depth, sample/channel semantics, compression, highest native resolution, orientation, physical pixel sizes, metadata and undefined/padded regions. On a small deterministic set spanning both standards and unknowns, compare extracted green pixels directly against an independent decode of the native plane. Use exact equality for integer samples; retain decoder/version/series/channel/crop coordinates and hashes. A rendered screenshot, TIFF extension, or lossless recompression is insufficient. Follow [OME's explicit dimension/compression semantics](https://bio-formats.readthedocs.io/en/stable/users/comlinetools/conversion.html). |
| **2. Review all 47 nuclear identities** | Present native crops and unchanged outlines, blinded to IOD and expected genome size. Record incomplete/excess boundary, overlap/debris, edge/clipping, halo/refraction and uncertain focus separately. Keep blank/unsure decisions distinct from acceptance and retain cell-linkage status. Include the full nuclear interior. Independently annotate a predeclared subset; retain disagreements. This applies [ESACP's visual segmentation control](https://www.classimed.de/esacimag.html). |
| **3. Fixed-mask reference-light diagnostics** | Freeze those same nuclear masks. Identify clean, non-nuclear background regions at predeclared positions. Compare central-field reference, verified local blank regions, and any genuinely matched dark/flat references without changing nuclei. Map shading and bright/dark clipping. Report reference availability and admissibility, not only a corrected number. Run the original expression alongside each proposed photometric branch. Where historical calibrations are absent, label background-derived branches as diagnostic assumptions; do not fabricate a dark frame or tune a reference to close the standard gap. |
| **4. Paired reimaging on retained slides, if available** | Use identifiable nuclei from both standards and representative unknowns; select by spatial/technical strata before seeing IOD. A practical starting design is 8–12 nuclei per slide where possible, across at least three spatially separated fields; this is a feasibility sample, not a literature minimum. Capture three frames without moving focus, then three after independent refocusing/repositioning. Add a short symmetric axial series with recorded z increments. Select the primary focal plane from blinded boundary/optical evidence, not highest IOD. Keep the remaining planes as focus sensitivity. Reimage matched nuclei under a suitable higher-NA objective only if the microscope permits it; apply physical-area or within-condition standard normalization. |
| **5. Response/stability check in the acquisition session** | With settings locked and illumination warmed/stable, collect clear-field and dark frames over an exposure range spanning actual nuclear/background signals. A nine-level, paired-frame screening sweep is a useful pilot, but not a full camera qualification. Document whether irradiance is stable; use known-transmission references if available to separate camera response from illumination variation. Record gain, white balance, gamma/LUT, compression, exposure, optical path, objective/NA, condenser configuration and cover glass. Report dark-subtracted response residuals and usable dynamic range. The design is motivated by [EMVA 1288 sections 6.5/6.9](https://www.emva.org/wp-content/uploads/EMVA1288Linear_4.0Release.pdf). |
| **6. Mask sensitivity after photometric characterization** | On scratch copies only, compare unchanged masks, independent complete-boundary annotations, and predeclared small erosions/dilations expressed in physical units. Measure native pixels; prevent neighboring labels/debris from entering the test region. Report added/removed ring area and its OD contribution, both with the frozen expression and incident-referenced model. Choose the biologically complete boundary from blinded raw evidence. The mask giving the desired standard median, lowest CV, or most convenient genome estimate is not an admissible selection rule. |
| **7. Spectral/refraction experiment, if informative channels survive** | If original RGB or multispectral data exist, retain each native channel and assess stain identity and camera response first. An absorption/refraction decomposition needs independently estimated components and validation on held-out fields or newly paired images. A single green observation cannot identify two unknown component concentrations without additional assumptions. Treat RGB/spectral unmixing as exploratory until it preserves repeatability and independently known DNA ratios. Do not import Thionin vectors into the magenta-Schiff assay. [Noureddine et al.](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26010057), [Ruifrok and Johnston](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11531144/). |
| **8. Chemical and absolute-scale follow-up** | Record actual fixative, fixation interval, drying, hydrolysis concentration/time/temperature, reagent identity/batch, washes, staining/mounting dates, mountant, storage and illumination histories. Same nominal protocol/run does not eliminate cell-type or achieved-treatment effects. Reimaging can characterize today's optical signal; it cannot reconstruct lost dye. If material remains, matched new preparations with independently characterized reference nuclei can test hydrolysis/stain response. A different staining run is a new experiment and cannot retroactively calibrate the old one without a validated linkage. |

Focus/refractive-index testing must distinguish the slide/coverslip interface from objective immersion. Preserve the existing mount; do not remove coverslips or replace mounting media during the first pilot. Record halos and their changes with focus and condenser conditions. The same-nucleus design allows optical-condition changes to be assessed without confusing them with between-nucleus DNA differences. Repeat images should be acquired quickly with controlled illumination history because [Dewse and Potter's fading experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/54953/) showed measurement illumination can itself change stain absorbance.

## QC, statistical sensitivity, and acceptance boundaries

Report the two designated standards separately throughout. The 47-nucleus census is the initial accounting denominator. List every eligibility, boundary, photometric, and human-review disposition, plus counts by field/slide. Describe all retained nuclei before any subset summary. A coefficient of variation can be a precision diagnostic for a verified homogeneous nuclear DNA class; it also mixes biology, field effects, and optical/mask error. Making CV small by deleting high/low IOD values is not validation.

Use the following distinct comparisons:

- **Capture repeatability:** within-nucleus variation of frames acquired without refocusing.
- **Refocus/reposition repeatability:** within-nucleus differences after an independent return to focus and field position.
- **Boundary reproducibility:** within-nucleus differences between blinded complete-boundary annotations, with their area and ring-OD changes.
- **Photometric sensitivity:** paired differences under frozen and physically justified light-reference/response branches, with identical masks.
- **Slide discrepancy:** separately summarized reviewed nuclei from 413 and 414, with field structure preserved. With one designated slide per specimen, specimen and slide effects are confounded; many nuclei cannot estimate independent slide replication.

Agreement plots should show paired differences against paired means and assess magnitude-dependent error; inspect log ratios where positive measurements show proportional error. Use within-nucleus and between-field/slide components rather than counting repeat frames as independent nuclei. Interval estimation should preserve nesting; a field-level bootstrap is conditional on these particular slides, and very few fields cannot support a precise distributional claim. These applications follow the distinction between association and agreement in [Bland and Altman, 1986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2868172/) and the repeated-unit treatment in [their 2007 paper](https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~mb55/meas/bland2007.pdf).

Before inspecting new IOD results, define the intended resolution of the study and a maximum tolerable technical contribution to it. The ESACP examples are useful comparison targets, not a reason to demand a predetermined number of accepted nuclei or to widen thresholds until they pass. A small feasibility pilot can locate a mechanism and estimate required replication; it cannot establish system accuracy merely by failing to detect a difference. Retain full distributions, paired changes, medians and untrimmed means, and explicitly flag sensitivity summaries.

For any later absolute calibration, write the nuclear-DNA-class factors explicitly. If target and reference are both verified 2C erythrocytes under comparable staining/measurement conditions, their 1C genome-size ratio can use the IOD ratio because the factor of two cancels. If their classes differ, that cancellation fails. Identify reference species, voucher/lineage, cell type and class, independent measurement method/value, and uncertainty before interpreting an absolute number. Neither the 0.978 conversion nor a 2c/4c instrument-linearity check supplies an independent species C-value. [Doležel et al., 2003](https://biotechcenter.web.illinois.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Dolezel-et-al.-2002.pdf).

For `C_target = R_IOD × C_reference`, the uncertainty budget must distinguish target sampling, shared denominator sampling, reference C-value, instrument response, boundary variation, and identifiable systematic effects. A common standard denominator creates correlated uncertainty across all targets; bootstrapping each target as if its calibration were independent understates that dependence. A nucleus-level interval is conditional on the retained slides and anchor. Unknown historical chemistry/response is an evidence gap, not zero variance. This application follows [NIST's full measurement-process and covariance guidance](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-appendix-law-propagation-uncertainty).

**Accepted measurement practice:** preserve original pixels; use incident light and calibrated response; integrate the complete isolated nucleus; review boundaries independently of the outcome; characterize focus, field position, stability and dynamic range; retain independent DNA reference/class evidence. **Exploratory recovery branches:** historical-image background estimation without matched dark/flat frames, mask dilation, sharpening/deconvolution, inferred response inversion, or spectral unmixing without assay-specific controls. Each may test a hypothesis, but none becomes a correction solely because standards agree better.

Software can correct demonstrated extraction mistakes, coordinate/label linkage mistakes, independently identified boundary mistakes, and physically calibrated detector/shading effects in new diagnostic outputs. It cannot recover uniquely clipped values, absent channels, unknown camera processing, nuclei outside the capture, chemically lost DNA/dye, or an absent independent C-value. The current evidence supports this pilot and a conditional sensitivity analysis. It does not yet support a recovered absolute-genome-size claim or replacement of a frozen denominator.
